Top Headlines
Economy · Congress

Kast's Mega Economic Reform Clears Congress — Constitutional Court Holds Showdown Hearings Today

Chile's biggest piece of economic legislation in years — President Kast's sweeping "Law for National Reconstruction and Economic and Social Development" — completed its full run through Congress this week when the Senate approved the executive's vetoes, stripping three provisions the opposition had inserted: anti-usury rules, a "right to financial forgiveness" on debts, and enhanced 30-day payment protections for small businesses. That technically ends the legislative process, but the bill still cannot be signed into law.

Today (August 13), Chile's Constitutional Court holds formal oral arguments on three unconstitutionality challenges filed by opposition lawmakers — think of it as the equivalent of a Supreme Court injunction fight. The TC has seven days to rule. The three pillars under challenge: a 20-year tax-stability freeze for investments over $350 million, fiscal compensation for environmental permits annulled by courts, and restrictions on the environmental impact review process. If the TC strikes any provisions, they're removed before promulgation; if it clears them, Kast can sign the bill into law. Corporate tax would drop from 27% to 23% over several years — though 56% of Chileans oppose the rate cut, per Cadem.

Security · Migration

ACOT Security Push Draws Debate as Deportations Double

President Kast's Agenda Against Organized Crime and Terrorism (ACOT), announced Aug. 5 via national broadcast, proposes roughly 30 bills, including a constitutional amendment to make security an explicit "duty of the state," extending the window for holding suspects caught in the act from 12 to 24 hours, permanent police deployment in the 50 highest-crime neighborhoods, and a special legal regime for organized crime kingpins — think RICO, but Chilean. Critics on the left say the package echoes mass-incarceration policies from the U.S. "tough on crime" era of the 1990s, raising civil-liberties concerns.

Meanwhile, data shows the migration crackdown is already producing results by the government's own metrics: deportations of undocumented migrants surged 176% in the first seven months of 2026 (1,330 executed orders), while irregular border crossings fell 33%. Opposition lawmakers question whether the pace prioritizes optics over due process.

Economy · Mining

Codelco Keeps All $2.4 Billion in 2025 Profits — First Time in Its History

The Kast government authorized Codelco — Chile's state copper company, which supplies a chunk of the world's copper supply — to reinvest 100% of its 2025 earnings, totaling $2.42 billion. It's the first time in the company's history that the government has allowed it to keep all its profits rather than channeling the majority to the treasury. The rationale: protect Codelco's credit rating and fund its massive ongoing production ramp-up without adding debt. Think of it as Congress finally letting the Pentagon keep its own procurement budget instead of going back to the Treasury every year.

Former Boric-era cabinet ministers have pushed back, arguing the decision diverts funds that would otherwise flow to social programs. The Kast administration counters that a financially stronger Codelco generates bigger long-term dividends for Chile.

Politics · Polling

Kast Ticks Up to 35% Approval, But Nearly Half of Chileans Now Say Country Is Going Backwards

The latest Criteria poll (conducted August 6) puts Kast's approval at 35% — a modest uptick — while disapproval holds at 52%. But the more striking number: the share of Chileans who feel the country is heading in the wrong direction jumped from 31% in March to 47% in August. That's a gap familiar to U.S. political watchers — a president's approval base can stabilize while broader public confidence erodes. The megarreforma has been Kast's signature win, yet its success in Congress hasn't translated into popular enthusiasm, with most Chileans still opposed to the corporate tax cuts that are the bill's centerpiece.

August Brings a Comet and a Lunar Eclipse — Both Visible From Santiago

It's a busy month for skywatchers. Comet P10/Tempel 2 is passing close enough to Earth to spot with a decent pair of binoculars on clear nights — something that happens once every several decades for this particular comet. A partial lunar eclipse is also scheduled later in August, visible from Chile's time zone. Santiago's winter air, especially after rainy days like today, can offer surprisingly sharp views once the clouds clear. BioBioChile has the full calendar with dates and viewing tips.

Opinion & Editorial
El Mostrador · Left-leaning · Aug 13

"Lo que las prioridades no pueden anticipar"

A science-policy column arguing that Chile's official R&D priority list — AI, energy transition, climate adaptation — has a structural blind spot: you can only prioritize what you already know exists. The author traces how technologies like GPS, online banking, and AI itself all depend on "useless" fundamental research done decades or centuries before anyone could have predicted the payoff. It's a gentle rebuke of the Kast administration's utilitarian approach to science funding, and an implicit argument for keeping Chile's research base broad even when budgets are tight. The column doesn't name Kast directly but the target is clear enough.

FAMFEST 2026 — International Family Theater Festival (Through Aug 23)

The 19th edition of Chile's premier family performing-arts festival is still running through August 23, with 33 shows spread across GAM, Teatro Mori, Teatro Municipal de Santiago, Teatro UC, and CEINA — plus venues in Valparaíso, Valdivia, and Rapa Nui. The lineup covers theater, circus, marionettes, opera, and dance, organized by age from sensory shows for toddlers up to productions for teenagers. Featured works include Cachalote, El País de las Maravillas (an Alice in Wonderland adaptation), and Golden: Las Guerreras K-pop. Over 22 shows include Chilean Sign Language (LSCh) interpretation. Tickets through each venue; some free and subsidized options exist.

🧵 Textiles

Estación Mapocho — Two Textile Memory Exhibitions (Ongoing)

Estación Mapocho (the grand old train station turned cultural center in central Santiago) is hosting two simultaneous exhibitions that use embroidery and weaving as vehicles for memory and identity — think of it as Chile's version of the AIDS Quilt, but rooted in Andean and folk traditions. The work spans indigenous textile techniques, community-made pieces linked to historical memory, and contemporary artists in dialogue with the tradition. Free or low-cost entry. Open through August.

Rock Indie

Fother Muckers — Así Se Hace (Announced; single Aug 17)

One of Chile's most influential indie rock bands is back after more than 15 years of silence, announcing Así Se Hace — their first studio album since the mid-2000s. The comeback is a genuinely big deal in the Chilean rock world: Fother Muckers helped define a generation of Santiago indie during the same era as Los Bunkers and Javiera Mena. The first single drops August 17 alongside the start of ticket sales for a return show. If you were into Chile's indie wave of the early 2000s or love angular guitar rock in the vein of Interpol or TV on the Radio, this is the comeback to watch.
