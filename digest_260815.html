Politics

Boric Admits "We Weren't Credible" on Security — and Calls on Chile's Left to Do the Same

Former President Gabriel Boric delivered one of the most striking public confessions by a recent Chilean leader this week, publicly stating that his government "was not credible" on public security and calling the early pension fund withdrawals — a politically popular move that drained Chile's pension system of tens of billions of dollars — "a major mistake." He made the remarks at the launch of a book by sociologist Eugenio Tironi, then doubled down in a post on X, writing that self-criticism by the left "must happen on our own initiative, in private and also in public."

The admission is politically significant. Boric held office from 2022 to 2026, broadly comparable to a two-term Democratic administration in US terms — his coalition swept in on a wave of protest energy from the 2019–20 "estallido" social uprising, then struggled to govern a polarized country. His public concession that they failed on crime provides rhetorical ammunition to the Kast government, which is currently pushing ACOT — a sweeping constitutional anti-crime package — through Congress. Former Interior Minister Carolina Tohá, who ran the security portfolio under Boric, pushed back, saying the prior government's "greatest achievement was building a cross-party agenda," a rebuttal that satisfied few.

New Poll: Kast Holds at 35% Approval, But 47% of Chileans Say the Country Is Going Backward

A new Criteria survey taken August 6 puts President Kast's approval at 35% — up one point from the prior month, with disapproval at 52%. The headline figure is less alarming for the government than a different number: the share of Chileans who believe the country is "going backward" has jumped from 31% in March to 47% now, while those who say Chile is "advancing" fell from 28% to 20%. That gap — a country that approves of its president at roughly the same rate as it approves of Congress, yet feels the national direction is worsening — mirrors the frustration pattern that sank Boric's government.

The main drivers appear to be economic anxiety rather than security dissatisfaction. Unemployment has been stuck above 8% for 42 consecutive months, reaching 9.4% in the April–June quarter. Inflation sits at 4.3% over the past 12 months, with gasoline up nearly 27% year-to-date. The government's bet is that passing ACOT will generate momentum and show legislative competence — but the public's mood is more about paychecks than police.

Security

ACOT Enters Senate as Government Pushes Christmas Deadline; Left Raises Civil Liberties Concerns

Chile's Agenda Contra el Crimen Organizado y el Terrorismo (ACOT) — the government's sweeping 31-bill anti-crime package, anchored by a constitutional amendment that would make public security a core state obligation — is now formally before the Senate after Kast signed the reform last Monday. The government has requested "maximum urgency" on the constitutional reform and is pressing for the full package to be passed by Christmas.

The debate is splitting the opposition. Some center-left senators — mindful of polls showing strong public appetite for tougher crime measures — are signaling openness to parts of ACOT. The Communist Party and the progressive left are raising alarms about the new "focused state of exception," which would allow military deployment in zones controlled by criminal organizations, arguing it's a backdoor toward authoritarian security powers. Think of it as a debate over whether to give the executive something like Patriot Act-level emergency powers, but targeted at gang-controlled neighborhoods.

Economy

42 Consecutive Months Above 8% Unemployment: Chile's Jobs Crisis Won't Budge

Chile's unemployment rate held at 9.4% in the April–June quarter, the country's 42nd straight month at or above 8% — the equivalent of the US never recovering to full employment since the early days of the Biden administration. Inflation rose 4.3% over the past 12 months, driven largely by fuel: gasoline is up ~27% this year, diesel up over 42%. The peso has found some relief as US inflation data eased pressure on the Federal Reserve, pushing the dollar rate down to 912 CLP on Thursday.

President Kast said this week he expects employment to improve in the fourth quarter as construction activity picks up and new environmental permitting rules reduce regulatory bottlenecks. Economists are cautious — a 2% growth target for 2026 looks increasingly out of reach, and some are flagging recession risk if global copper demand softens further.

Kast Projects Job Rebound, Approves New Housing Subsidies; Ex-Boric Ministers Push Back on Economic Record

President Kast this week held a working meeting with the Public Works and Transport minister to accelerate infrastructure projects, and signed off on a new housing subsidy expansion — raising the cap on eligible homes to 6,000 UF (roughly $200,000 USD) and extending the program through May 2028, adding 30,000 new slots. The move is designed to restart Chile's stalled construction sector, which typically accounts for about 8% of GDP.

Meanwhile, former ministers under Gabriel Boric continued a coordinated critique of the Kast government's economic decisions, targeting the 40-hour workweek rollback, revised CASEN poverty survey methodology, and Codelco strategy. The back-and-forth mirrors the pattern of US administrations attacking each other's signature policies — with both sides claiming the economy's problems belong to the other's watch.

A Partial Lunar Eclipse Will Be Visible from Santiago on the Night of August 27–28

Mark the date: on the night of August 27 into the early hours of August 28, Santiago will have a front-row seat to a partial lunar eclipse. NASA says Chile and the rest of South America will have among the best viewing conditions on the planet for this event — no equipment needed, just a clear sky. Mid-August through September is historically one of the better windows for stargazing in Santiago as winter fronts begin to thin out. August's astronomical calendar also included Comet P10/Tempel 2 passing close to Earth at the start of the month, and the comet was visible with binoculars from the southern hemisphere.

First Major Snowfall in Over a Decade Blankets Loncoche in La Araucanía

The Araucanía town of Loncoche, about 700 km south of Santiago, woke up to snow accumulation on August 8 that locals say is the heaviest since at least 2014. La Araucanía is Chile's version of Appalachia — historically underinvested and often overlooked in the national conversation — but the region's Mapuche communities, farmers, and small towns shared photos that went briefly viral as Chileans in Santiago marveled at the wintery contrast to their fog-bound capital. No structural damage was reported; mostly the internet loved it.

"Crítica, autocrítica y política"

Carlos Durán — a political scientist who served as chief of staff to Gabriel Boric — argues that the left's self-criticism moment, while necessary, risks becoming performative if it isn't translated into a genuine rethinking of strategy and organization. Boric's public admission of failure on security is a starting point, Durán writes, but admitting you were wrong is not the same as knowing what to do differently. The left must reconnect with voters who prioritized order and economic stability — and that requires listening to those voters, not just giving a more polished version of the same speech. A bracing piece of internal left-wing honesty, lean center-left in framing.

Los Tres — XCLNT (2026) · Original Lineup, Abbey Road, 26 Years Later

Los Tres' eighth studio album XCLNT — released April 10 and still generating steady press coverage — is one of the Chilean music stories of the year. The band recorded it at Abbey Road Studio 2 in London in July 2025, reuniting the original four members (Álvaro Henríquez, Roberto Lindl, Ángel Parra, Francisco Molina) for the first time in 26 years. The album's three singles — "Cantar y amar," "Como llegaste te vas," and "INRI" — are accompanied by videos made with Punkrobot, the studio that won Chile's first Oscar for the animated short Historia de un oso. Today at the Día del Vinilo (GAM), the band releases "La Sangre en el Cuerpo" as a limited 7" single — pick it up if you go. For fans of: classic Chilean rock, Los Prisioneros, Café Tacvba.
