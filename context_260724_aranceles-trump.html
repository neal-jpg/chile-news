Trump's Tariffs on Chile: What's Happening

Chile and the United States have had a Free Trade Agreement (FTA) since 2004 — one of the first the US signed in Latin America. Under that deal, virtually all tariffs between the two countries were eliminated. For two decades, Chilean exporters sending wine, salmon, fresh fruit, or avocados to the US faced zero or near-zero import duties. That arrangement is now effectively suspended by Trump-era executive action.

How this tariff works. The Trump administration has used Section 301 of the Trade Act (the same authority used for China tariffs) to impose "reciprocal" tariffs on dozens of countries, including traditional allies with FTAs. A separate executive order targets countries the administration says have failed to "adequately combat forced labor" in their supply chains — the theory being that goods produced under coercive labor conditions unfairly undercut American producers. Chile is listed in this second tranche. The rate went from 10% (the base reciprocal tariff imposed earlier in 2026) to 12.5% effective today.

The American analogy. Think of it like the US slapping tariffs on Canada during NAFTA — technically the FTA still exists on paper, but the executive is imposing duties that override it. Chile's government, like Canada's in 2018, is contesting the legality and negotiating for exemptions, but the tariffs are in effect immediately. The "forced labor" charge stings the most because Chile has strong labor protections, and Santiago flatly rejects the premise.

What's actually affected. Copper — Chile's biggest export — is carved out of this order (it's under a separate Section 232 national-security investigation). The goods that take the hit are agricultural: wine, salmon, table grapes, blueberries, avocados, and processed foods. These sectors employ hundreds of thousands of Chileans, and their competitiveness in the US market depends on price. A 12.5% tariff is not devastating on its own, but it erodes the margin that made Chilean fruit and wine attractive to US buyers.
