Top Headlines
Politics

Congress passes Kast's sweeping economic reform — vetoes and court fights still ahead

After a grinding six-month battle, Chile's Senate voted 27–22 on the final contested article of President Kast's flagship "Economic and Social Reconstruction Law," clearing the bill from Congress entirely. The reform is Chile's biggest supply-side overhaul in decades: it cuts the corporate income tax from 27% to 23% (think a Republican tax-cut bill), exempts first-home purchases from the equivalent of sales tax, and guarantees tax-rate stability for large investors. Kast addressed the nation by cadena nacional — Chile's version of a presidential prime-time broadcast — declaring "Chile begins to grow again."

But the fight is far from over. The government announced it will file four presidential vetoes on Wednesday, targeting provisions Congress added over the administration's objections: a ban on compound interest charges, a 30-day payment guarantee for small businesses, and a financial-history "right to oblivion." The opposition, meanwhile, has opened three fronts at Chile's Constitutional Court (think the US Supreme Court, but specifically for constitutional challenges) challenging tax-invariability clauses and other contested elements. Former Economy Minister Nicolás Grau — a Boric-era cabinet member turned opposition voice — warned the reform "won't make a great difference" for ordinary Chileans and raises long-term fiscal risks given the reduced corporate tax base.

Emergency · Santiago

Toxic chemical explosion in Quilicura forces school closures across the commune

A massive fire erupted Tuesday night at Panimex Química — a factory producing raw materials for plastics and paints, located next to Route 5 Norte in Quilicura (northwest Santiago). Explosions and flames over 15 meters sent plumes of toxic ammonia and chemical smoke into surrounding residential neighborhoods. By Wednesday morning, authorities confirmed all schools in Quilicura suspended for the day. Firefighters from multiple companies, including specialized Hazmat units, battled through the night. Workers had evacuated and no injuries were reported, but the neighborhood remains under a health advisory.

Notably, this is the third major fire at the Panimex facility — previous incidents occurred in 2014 and 2017 — raising pointed questions about whether the plant was storing hazardous materials safely and why regulatory follow-through after the earlier incidents was apparently insufficient.

Budget

Kast's budget plan quietly proposes axing 401 social programs — including elderly care

Finance Minister Jorge Quiroz circulated an internal memo recommending that the 2027 national budget discontinue 142 government programs across 22 ministries and cut 15% or more from 260 others. The combined savings target is roughly $6 billion. Among the proposed reductions: 427 billion pesos (about $450 million) cut from nine programs serving over 900,000 elderly adults. Health, Education, and Science ministries face the largest proposed reductions. Forty of the targeted programs directly contradict promises Kast made on the campaign trail.

The opposition reacted sharply, calling it a betrayal of Kast's working-class voters. The government walked the memo back, with Quiroz insisting it was "guidance for evaluation, not a decree" and that "no program will be cut or ended." Critics note the timing is uncomfortable: with the megarreforma reducing corporate tax revenue, the budget math has to balance somewhere — and Quiroz's framing suggests social spending is first in the crosshairs.

Former president Gabriel Boric, whose four-year term ended in March, has apparently discovered the joys of post-presidential social media. Opinion & Editorial
The Clinic · Left-leaning

"A legislative victory is not a political victory"

Political scientist Marco Moreno argues that Kast's congressional win on the megarreforma is real but narrow. The reform passed on right-bloc votes, not through genuine coalition-building, and its most controversial clauses face Constitutional Court challenges the government may not survive. Moreno's core claim: winning a bill without winning public opinion means implementation headaches ahead — and an emboldened opposition that now has a judicial avenue to chip away at the reform piecemeal. Chile's right, he contends, may have passed a law but hasn't yet made the moral-economic case for it to a skeptical country.

La Tercera · Center-right

"With this law, Chile begins to grow again"

La Tercera's center-right coverage and the Kast government's own framing hold that the megarreforma is a supply-side turning point on par with Chile's post-Pinochet liberalization era — and not unlike Reagan-era US tax cuts in philosophy. Finance Minister Quiroz argues the bill was rewritten dozens of times to accommodate congressional concerns, making what emerged balanced rather than extreme. Lower corporate taxes mean more investment; more investment means more jobs; more jobs mean higher wages. The question the coverage leaves open: whether this logic holds when global copper prices — Chile's real economic engine — can override any domestic policy with a single commodity swing.
