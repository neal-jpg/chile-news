Politics

The International Right Descends on Santiago — and It's Splitting Kast's Coalition

Chile's capital hosts the V Regional Meeting of Foro Madrid on Thursday and Friday (September 3–4), an international summit organized by Spain's Vox party through its Fundación Disenso think tank. President Kast will open the conference alongside Argentina's President Javier Milei; Vox leader Santiago Abascal gives the welcome; delegates from more than 25 countries attend. The venue is the San Carlos de Apoquindo convention center in Las Condes.

The event is unprecedented: no previous Chilean president has hosted Foro Madrid from office. It has cracked the governing coalition — Evópoli and Renovación Nacional both officially distanced themselves, saying the Foro doesn't represent their vision. Critics, including legal scholars and former constitutional judges, argue the Foro's founding document (the Carta de Madrid, which Kast signed in 2021) contains principles at odds with liberal democratic norms. The opposition has organized a parallel counter-event, "Democracia Siempre," for September 3 at Hotel Fundador, bringing together parties from the Communist Party to Christian Democracy.

Congress

Kast Backs Off Urgency on Security Reform as Senate Votes Remain Elusive

On Monday the Kast government quietly dropped the legislative urgency on its flagship security reform from "suma urgencia" (15-day deadline) to "urgencia simple" (30 days), buying time for negotiations that have so far failed. The reform would create a new "state of exception" — similar to a US president declaring a national emergency, but with far broader powers to suspend civil liberties — that could run for up to 8 months without prior Congressional approval.

Even within the ruling coalition, Senator Matías Walker called the state-of-exception provision "madness — the way democracies die." Renovación Nacional lawmakers are now pushing to strip that provision from the reform entirely. The Senate debate is expected to begin the second week of September. The government needs 26 Senate votes to pass a constitutional amendment; it currently has 25 at best.

Politics · Memory

Chile's 53rd: Opposition Prepares Dense Commemoration Amid Foro Madrid Tensions

September 11 marks the 53rd anniversary of the 1973 military coup that killed President Salvador Allende and launched 17 years of Pinochet dictatorship. The opposition has loaded the next two weeks: "Democracia Siempre" dialogue on September 3, a tribute at Allende's statue outside La Moneda on September 4 (the anniversary of his 1970 electoral victory), and a march on September 11.

For many Chileans — and especially for the left — the convergence of the Foro Madrid summit (bringing together far-right international leaders critical of liberal democracy) with the coup anniversary week is historically charged. Critics call it deeply tone-deaf; government supporters say the opposition is weaponizing history to avoid debating policy. The commemoration is expected to draw large crowds, especially this year.

Economy

Quiroz: Chile Dodged Recession — But the Second Half Must Deliver

Finance Minister Quiroz has been making the rounds to point out that Chile technically avoided recession after the June IMACEC showed 2.4% expansion — stopping the five-month growth slide that had economists worried. (Think of IMACEC as Chile's monthly GDP equivalent, the figure the Federal Reserve would watch in the US.) But to hit the government's 1.2% full-year target, the economy needs to grow 2.5–2.6% in each of the next two quarters.

Industrial production dropped sharply in July — mining took the biggest hit — and US tariffs continue to weigh on exports. The National Reconstruction Law, passed after this winter's destructive storms, is supposed to inject investment, but large public works projects typically lag before spending actually flows. Quiroz remains upbeat publicly; most independent economists are more cautious.

Culture · Offbeat

Chile Didn't Make the World Cup — But Thousands of Babies Are Named Messi and Mbappé Now

Chile didn't qualify for the 2026 World Cup (held across the US, Canada, and Mexico this summer), but that didn't stop the country from going football-mad. Civil registry data shows a measurable spike in parents naming newborns "Messi" and "Mbappé" during and after the tournament. Researchers who track the data say the trend follows previous World Cups — "Ronaldo" saw a similar wave after 2014. The names are most common in working-class comunas, where World Cup fervor runs hottest.

Opinion & Editorial
La Tercera · Center · Jurist Marisol Peña

"El peligro de una democracia 'iliberal'"

Former president of Chile's Constitutional Tribunal (the Chilean equivalent of a retired Supreme Court Justice) Marisol Peña argues that the Carta de Madrid — the founding document Kast signed and on which Foro Madrid is based — contains principles fundamentally incompatible with liberal democratic governance: skepticism of independent courts, media freedom, and minority rights. Hosting such a forum from the presidential office, she writes, gives illiberal ideas a seal of state legitimacy they don't deserve and sends a dangerous signal ten days before the coup anniversary.

El Líbero · Right-Leaning · Editorial

"La izquierda y sus dobles estándares"

An El Líbero editorial this week pushes back on the left's outrage over Foro Madrid, arguing that opposition parties hosted international socialist forums for years without equivalent scrutiny, and that "Democracia Siempre" is a cynical power play rather than a genuine defense of democratic values. The column contends that conservatives gathering internationally is no different from the left doing so, and that Kast is simply exercising his right to convene ideological allies — as every government does. The real issue, the column argues, is that the left can't accept losing elections.

Asia Menor — "Vi al Diablo"

Indie Alt-Rock

Hotel Records — Compilado Chile 2026

The Santiago-based indie platform released its fourth annual snapshot of the Chilean underground — 15 artists chosen from over 100 applicants. The range is genuinely wide: 90s-style guitar rock, shoegaze, aggressive punk, introspective folk, and some experimental outliers. Standouts include Club de Campo, Maldito Freak, Hole In Canvas, OTO, and Big Sur. Think of it as a long-form college radio sampler for the Chilean indie scene. Free on Spotify, YouTube, and other platforms — a solid entry point if you want to know what's being made right now.

Hip Hop

Roce de Mente — VERTIENTES, CAMINOS, SERPIENTES

The hip-hop trio of Maitezaz, Heilow, and Balta Carmona released their debut full-length, a record that wears its 90s US influences openly — De La Soul warmth, J Dilla-adjacent production — while the lyrics are firmly Chilean, dealing with barrio life, friendship, and everyday hustle. This isn't chart hip-hop; the spirit is closer to underground East Coast boom-bap than to reggaeton. An encouraging debut from a scene that's been developing its own identity outside the commercial mainstream.
