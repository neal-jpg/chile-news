Economy

Chile Enters Technical Recession for First Time Since 2023

The Banco Central confirmed Tuesday that GDP fell 0.2% in April–June 2026, following a downwardly revised 0.3% contraction in Q1 — making Chile's first technical recession in three years official. Two consecutive quarters of negative GDP growth is the same definition the US uses informally (the formal US arbiter is the NBER, which looks at more factors, but two quarters is what everyone calls a recession in practice).

Copper mining, Chile's biggest export, dropped 6.4% as global prices softened and major operations underperformed. Construction fell 3.2%, investment was essentially flat, and while household consumption managed a 1.2% gain, it couldn't offset the drag from mining and exports. Analysts surveyed by the Banco Central have cut their full-year 2026 growth forecast to roughly 1%.

President Kast, who took office in March pledging economic reactivation, said in a radio interview on August 10 that recovery would come in Q4. His corporate tax cut package — Chile's version of supply-side economics — cleared Congress in July, but has yet to produce visible results. Unemployment sits at its highest level since 2021.

Security

Kast Sends Crime Constitutional Reform to Senate Under 15-Day Fast-Track

The government submitted eight constitutional amendments to the Senate Monday under "suma urgencia" — the Chilean equivalent of a legislative emergency clock requiring a floor vote within 15 days, similar to how Congress uses "expedited procedures" on must-pass bills. The centerpiece is a new "public security state of exception" that would let the president deploy the military against organized crime for up to 120 days, renewable.

Additional measures expand police powers at borders, create a national criminal organizations registry, stiffen penalties for recruiting minors into gangs, modify the anti-barricade law, and broaden grounds for expelling irregular migrants. Kast has said he wants the reforms passed by December. The left and center-left opposition, which controls a significant congressional bloc, have raised concerns about militarizing domestic policing.

Politics

Kast Approval Frozen at 35% as Pessimism Deepens Across Chile

The Criteria weekly tracking poll (August 16) put President Kast at 35% approval — unchanged for the third consecutive week — while disapproval climbed three points to 55%. The numbers beneath the headline are grimmer: the share of Chileans who believe the country is "going backwards" has surged from 31% in March to 47% now, and the "hope" index has fallen from 53% in February to just 36%. For US context, think of it as an economy-driven presidential drag of the kind that sank George H.W. Bush's reelection in 1992 — a president personally liked but held responsible for a downturn he didn't cause but couldn't reverse fast enough.

El Mostrador · Left-leaning · Aug 16

"The Emergency Kast Keeps Treating as a Surprise"

This column argues that Chile's economic downturn was entirely predictable — copper prices, global headwinds, and weak domestic investment were all flashing warning signs for months — yet the Kast government has reacted to each piece of bad news as though it arrived without warning. The critique: ideological commitment to supply-side tax cuts has blinded the administration to the structural nature of the slowdown, meaning its prescriptions can't fix what it won't diagnose. A left-of-center argument that the problem isn't just cyclical bad luck but a failure of economic framing.

Ex-Ante · Right-leaning · Roberto Cases

"Security: A Necessary Agenda for Long-Term Growth"

Roberto Cases argues that Kast's ACOT security push isn't just law-and-order politics — it's economic policy. Crime costs Chile an estimated 2.1% of GDP annually in direct damages, insurance, lost foreign investment, and capital flight. The column frames the constitutional reform as the supply-side complement to the tax cut package: neither alone is enough, but stable and safe conditions are a prerequisite for investment to actually materialize. A right-leaning case for why security and growth are the same agenda, not competing ones.
