Trade / Diplomacy

US Trade Envoy in Santiago for High-Stakes Tariff Talks

US Deputy Trade Representative Jeffrey Goettman arrived in Santiago Monday for two days of high-level bilateral negotiations — the most senior American trade official to visit Chile since Washington imposed a 12.5% surcharge on a large portion of Chilean exports in late July. Chile's undersecretariat for international economic relations, Paula Estévez, led the Chilean side at the Foreign Ministry. Both governments agreed to keep talking in coming weeks, signaling no quick fix but a serious channel is open.

The 12.5% tariff — imposed under a US forced-labor law enforcement action — costs Chilean exporters an estimated $212 million. About 60% of Chilean exports to the US remain exempt, including copper cathodes, lithium carbonate, and most fresh produce. Chile has not signed Washington's proposed "reciprocal trade agreement" and is instead arguing that its existing free-trade deal with the US should be honored. Think of it as Chile trying to enforce the fine print of a contract the other party is trying to rewrite.

Politics

Kast's Security Reform Stalls — Even His Own Coalition Is Balking

President Kast's proposed constitutional overhaul of public security — which would let the executive restrict civil liberties for up to 240 days without congressional approval — has run into fierce opposition from all sides. The left demanded he withdraw the bill entirely, with one senator declaring: "If you're not illiberal, prove it with actions." More striking: Evópoli, a party inside Kast's own right-wing bloc, warned the powers were too broad. "We'd never want these in an opposition's hands," one Evópoli senator said.

The government quietly pulled the bill's legislative urgency Monday, buying time. Kast denied the "illiberal" label and opened the door to modifications. The stalemate is a reminder that his governing bloc controls 76 of 155 lower-house seats — enough to govern narrowly but not to steamroll on constitutional matters, which require supermajority thresholds (like the US needing two-thirds of the Senate for a treaty).

Economy / Law

Economic Reform Law Clears Congress, Waits for Constitutional Court

Kast's signature economic legislation — the "Plan de Reconstrucción Nacional y Desarrollo Económico y Social" — passed Congress on August 4 but cannot become law until Chile's Constitutional Court rules on challenges to specific provisions. The reform cuts corporate income taxes (think: a Chilean version of the 2017 US Tax Cuts and Jobs Act), guarantees tax stability for investment projects above $50 million, and funds rebuilding in the wildfire-hit regions of Ñuble, Biobío, and Valparaíso. The government says it could push GDP growth from 2.5% today toward 4% by end of term.

Today Kast holds a ceremony at the Plaza de la Constitución to deliver new patrol vehicles to the PDI (Chile's FBI equivalent), showcasing the security investment side of his agenda while the economic reform sits in legal limbo.

Culture

Condorito Turns 75 — Chile's Beloved Comic Rooster Gets an Exhibition

The rooster-man from Pelotillehue — Chile's answer to Charlie Brown meets Bart Simpson, but ruder — turns 75 this year. Created by cartoonist René Ríos "Pepo" in 1949, Condorito has been a fixture in Chilean households across every class and generation, the rare pop-culture figure that unites grandparents and grandchildren. A free retrospective exhibition at Mercado Urbano de Tobalaba in Las Condes celebrates his anniversary with iconic panels, classic characters like Pepe Cortisona and Yayita, and memorabilia spanning seven decades. It's a nice, low-key local-history moment at a time when the national news is anything but.

"The 'Illiberal' Signals in Kast's Security Reform That Worry Congress"

El Mostrador's analysis examines provisions in Kast's constitutional security overhaul that would allow the executive to restrict civil liberties for up to 240 days without a legislative vote — longer than any comparable emergency measure in the region. The piece cites political scientists who draw parallels to constitutional changes under Hungary's Orbán, calling it a red flag in a country with living memory of Pinochet's authoritarian emergency decrees. The argument: well-intentioned security goals don't require dismantling the checks that protect everyone when the next government arrives with different intentions. Center-left outlet, critical across the political spectrum.

Rock Indie

Los Tres — XCLNT

The biggest Chilean rock album in decades: Los Tres reunited their original lineup — Álvaro Henríquez, Ángel Parra, Titae Lindl, and Pancho Molina — for the first time in 26 years and recorded XCLNT at Abbey Road Studios in London. Released in April and still generating coverage all summer, the album is a raw, emotionally direct return to the rawness that made their 1990s records essential. For fans of: the Pixies, Caifanes, or anyone who gets why Nirvana's "In Utero" sounds better than "Nevermind." Los Tres are Chile's equivalent of Pearl Jam — a band that defined a generation and whose reunion carries real cultural weight.
