Economy · Politics

Kast's Mega Tax Reform Clears Congress — Corporate Rate Drops From 27% to 23%

The Chilean Senate voted 27-22 Tuesday to approve the final pending article of Kast's flagship "Ley de Reconstrucción y Desarrollo Económico y Social," sending his signature tax reform package one procedural step from becoming law. The bill slashes the corporate income tax rate from 27% to 23% by 2029, offers a VAT exemption on first-home sales, and creates a 15% tax credit for each formally employed worker — roughly the Chilean equivalent of a US employer payroll tax cut bundled with a tax reform bill.

The bill now returns to the Chamber of Deputies to ratify the Senate's amendments before going to Kast for signature. The government also announced vetoes targeting three opposition-inserted provisions: financial amnesty on old debts, a ban on compound interest, and a 30-day payment guarantee for small businesses. The opposition has vowed to challenge the reform at the Constitutional Court (Chile's Supreme Court equivalent for constitutional questions). The independent fiscal watchdog called the package a "net negative" for public finances; the IMF flagged it as ambitious and warned additional efforts would be needed to meet debt targets.

Security · Politics

Kast Launches Anti-Crime Blitz: Constitutional Amendment, 30+ Bills, Permanent Police in 50 Neighborhoods

President Kast unveiled his most sweeping law-enforcement agenda to date via a national broadcast Wednesday — the Agenda contra el Crimen Organizado y el Terrorismo (ACOT). The centerpiece is a constitutional reform to explicitly enshrine the state's duty to protect public safety, giving police broader legal cover (think a Chilean equivalent of a constitutional amendment adding "law and order" to the government's enumerated duties). Other measures: extending the window police can hold a suspect without charge from 12 to 24 hours, permanently stationing officers in the 50 highest-crime neighborhoods, and targeting criminal organizations as whole enterprises rather than chasing individual arrests — similar to how US RICO laws go after the mob, not just street dealers.

The backdrop: Chile has around 900,000 people unemployed, and critics from the left argue Kast is using crime as a distraction from the jobs crisis. Some opposition legislators said it was "a concrete strategy"; others were blunter — "we've heard too many promises."

Foreign Policy

Chile Quits the Non-Aligned Movement After 55 Years, in a Sharp Diplomatic Pivot Toward Washington

Chile formally withdrew from the Non-Aligned Movement (NAM), the 120-nation bloc of countries — mostly from the Global South — that was founded during the Cold War for nations that didn't want to pick sides between the US and the USSR. Chile had been a member since 1971, when Salvador Allende joined as a statement of socialist independence. The Kast government sent a written withdrawal notice to the United Nations, citing the organization's "different historical context."

The move is being read as a deliberate alignment with Donald Trump's revival of the Monroe Doctrine — the US's 200-year-old claim to a special "sphere of influence" over Latin America. Critics note Kast has also pulled support for Michelle Bachelet's UN Secretary-General bid and severed ties with the UN committee on Palestinian rights. Former diplomats warn the exit could hurt Valparaíso's bid to host an international secretariat and cool relations with Bolivia.

Economy

Economy Grows 2.4% in June, Narrowly Dodging a Technical Recession

Chile's IMACEC (the monthly economic activity index, roughly equivalent to a monthly GDP reading) rose 2.4% year-over-year in June, snapping five consecutive months of contraction and pulling the country back from the brink of a technical recession (two or more consecutive quarters of negative growth). Copper mining was the main driver, up 9.8% annually as global demand stayed firm. The Central Bank still projects full-year 2026 growth of just 1%–1.75% — below the pace needed to bring unemployment down meaningfully. Full second-quarter GDP data drops August 18, which will be the definitive verdict on whether Chile officially entered or escaped recession.

Winter Storm's Toll: 2 Dead, Thousands Without Power Across Central Chile

The frontal system that drenched Chile's center-south over the past week left 2 people dead, 2,476 affected, and more than 5,500 homes damaged or destroyed. At peak, nearly 80,000 customers lost power; as of yesterday, tens of thousands remained without electricity across the Maule, Ñuble, Biobío, Araucanía, and Los Lagos regions. The storm is the same system bringing rain to Santiago this morning — it's moving out, but repair work in southern communities will continue for days.

Opinion & Editorial
"Flexibilidad sin contrapesos: lo que la agenda laboral de Kast copia (y omite) de Europa"
El Mostrador · Left-leaning · August 5

The column argues that Kast's proposed labor flexibility reforms — including replacing traditional severance pay with an "anytime" indemnification fund — are borrowed from European deregulation playbooks, but without the robust unemployment safety nets that make those European models work. In Denmark or the Netherlands, flexibility is paired with generous benefits and active retraining programs; what Kast proposes, the author says, is the deregulation without the cushion — leaving workers exposed while companies gain. For US readers: imagine if Congress killed severance law, raised the cap on at-will firing, but left food stamps and job training programs untouched — or weakened. That's the critique.
