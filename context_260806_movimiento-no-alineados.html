The Non-Aligned Movement — what it is and why Chile just quit

The Non-Aligned Movement (NAM, or MNOAL in Spanish) was born in 1961, at the height of the Cold War, as a club for countries that didn't want to choose sides between the United States and the Soviet Union. Think of it as the original "don't tread on me" coalition — 120-plus nations from Asia, Africa, Latin America and the Caribbean that insisted on an independent foreign policy.

Chile joined in 1971 under Salvador Allende, the world's first democratically elected Marxist president. For Allende, membership was a statement: Chile was charting its own socialist path and wouldn't be dictated to by Washington. The membership survived every subsequent government — military dictatorship, center-left, center-right — for 55 consecutive years.

The American analogy: imagine if the US had been a member of a bloc designed to stay neutral between NATO and the Warsaw Pact. Staying in after the Cold War ended would be mostly symbolic, but quitting still sends a message.

Cuba remains one of the NAM's most vocal members, which is exactly why President Kast's decision to leave is loaded. Critics, including opposition legislators and former diplomats, say the move signals that Chile is aligning itself with the United States under President Trump — specifically with Trump's revival of the Monroe Doctrine, the 200-year-old doctrine that treats Latin America as America's backyard. One headline put it plainly: Kast is "aligning Chile with the renewed Monroe Doctrine of Donald Trump."

The government's stated reason is more prosaic: the NAM "was created as a political movement in a different global context" and no longer reflects current realities. Critics counter that the real subtext is ideological — distancing Chile from the Venezuela-Cuba orbit and signaling a hard pivot toward the West. Practical concerns: diplomats warn it could hurt Valparaíso's bid to host an international secretariat, and may cool relations with Bolivia at a moment when border cooperation matters.
