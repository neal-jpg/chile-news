Economy · Congress · Law

Kast's Mega Tax-Cut Is Almost Law — But Three Constitutional Challenges Stand Between It and Full Effect

Congress approved 27 of 28 modifications to Kast's sweeping economic package — the Law for National Reconstruction and Economic Social Development — on July 21, delivering his largest legislative win since taking office in March. The centerpiece: a gradual corporate income-tax cut from 27% to 23% over several years, putting Chile closer to OECD norms. The reform also locks in tax stability for investors (shielding them from future government changes) and gives companies the right to seek fiscal compensation when environmental disputes halt a project. One provision is still in a joint committee; everything else passed.

The center-left opposition has now filed three separate challenges at Chile's Tribunal Constitucional — think of it as a cross between the US Supreme Court and a constitutional referee for legislation. The three fronts: the tax-invariability clause, the indemnity for blocked environmental permits, and restrictions on how disputes move through the environmental review process. If the TC strikes any of these provisions, Kast's prize legislation could be narrowed before it takes full effect. A key vote could be decided by TC president María Pía Silva, whose position has been closely watched by both sides.

Economy · Labor

Unemployment Numbers Out Today — Minister Pre-Warned of "Bad News"

Chile's INE (national statistics institute, equivalent to the US Bureau of Labor Statistics) is releasing updated labor market figures today, July 31. Economy Minister Daniel Mas got ahead of it Tuesday, warning that "probably this and next month will bring bad news for the labor market" — he cited winter seasonal slowdowns and economic disruption from June's devastating storms in Coquimbo and Atacama. The March–May unemployment rate already stood at 9.4%, the highest in five years, with Chile stuck at or above 8% for 38 consecutive months. About 980,000 Chileans are currently unemployed and actively seeking work; broader measures including discouraged workers push effective underemployment to an estimated 13.2%.

Think of it like the US hitting Great Recession unemployment levels — but repeatedly, with no V-shaped recovery in sight. The political irony is sharp: Congress just passed a pro-investor tax reform while labor markets continue to deteriorate.

Weather · Nacional

Frontal System Disrupts Chile — 58 Communes Without Classes, Mountain Parks Closed

A powerful frontal system sweeping through central and southern Chile today has forced class cancellations in 58 communes spanning Atacama, Ñuble, Biobío, and Aysén. Conaf closed Parque Nacional Laguna del Laja preventively through Friday, with forecasts showing intense precipitation, snow in the precordillera, and gusts potentially exceeding 70 km/h in mountain sectors. Santiago is seeing steady rain but no class suspensions as of this morning.

The context adds weight to the disruption: the same coastal-front pattern that caused June's catastrophic storms — which killed people, destroyed 1,500 homes in Coquimbo, and cut off 40,000 residents — has made officials significantly more cautious about preventive action. Government reconstruction spending for that earlier disaster is estimated at over US$500 million and has not yet been fully allocated.

Justice · Military

Supreme Court: Army Corporal's Death Must Be Probed by Civilian Prosecutors, Not the Military

In a 3–2 split ruling issued Thursday, Chile's Supreme Court ordered the civilian Ministerio Público (the federal prosecutor's office) to take over the investigation into the March death of Army Corporal Carlos Palacios, who drowned in an unauthorized swimming exercise ordered by officers near Punta Arenas. Chile's forensic service determined the cause: asphyxia by submersion. A captain and sergeant involved had already been detained by military prosecutors; the ruling transfers jurisdiction entirely to civilian authorities.

The ruling matters beyond the Palacios case. Chile's military justice system has historically resisted civilian oversight of incidents inside bases — a dynamic similar to debates in the US over the Uniform Code of Military Justice (UCMJ) vs. federal courts in cases of alleged military abuse. Chile's human rights institute (INDH) brought the original complaint. The narrow 3–2 margin signals the question of military vs. civilian jurisdiction remains deeply contested in the courts.

Politics · Government

Kast Sends Congress a Constitutional Amendment: Positive Drug Test Means Removal From Office

After firing Finance Ministry Undersecretary Juan Pablo Rodríguez earlier this month for failing a drug test, President Kast announced a constitutional amendment that would require mandatory, publicly released periodic drug tests for ministers, senators, deputies, governors, and mayors. A positive result would be a new formal cause for removal from office — think of it as the Chilean equivalent of adding "tests positive for drugs" as grounds for congressional impeachment. Kast: "There cannot be two viewpoints because the risk to our democracy is very high." Some allies worry the proposal is politically reactive and could be weaponized; critics on the left call it authoritarian. The amendment still requires a two-thirds congressional majority to pass.

Interesting & Offbeat

Kast Just Won His Big Economic Reform — Now His Coalition Is Fighting Over Abortion

Within days of passing the megarreforma, Kast's governing alliance fractured — over an entirely different front. National Libertarian Party deputies (led by Johannes Kaiser, Chile's most provocative far-right libertarian) pushed three abortion-related bills: one requiring women to hear the fetal heartbeat before the procedure, another criminalizing sellers of Misoprostol. The Kast government very publicly distanced itself from all three. Kaiser fired back: after four months in power, he argued, the government "can't walk and chew gum at the same time." Republicans replied that the values agenda "is not a priority."

It's a snapshot of a tension familiar to any American who watched the Tea Party era: a fiscally conservative president trying to focus on economic results while culture-war allies push social issues that complicate the coalition. In Chile the parties involved are the libertarian Partido Republicano, the conservative Republicanos, and the UDI — and they're not even fully aligned with each other on the scope of what to push.

Earth Ran Out of 2026's Resources Yesterday — Chile's Copper and Lithium Sit at the Intersection

July 30 was Earth Overshoot Day 2026 — the point at which humanity exhausted all the natural resources the planet can sustainably renew in a single year. For the remaining five months of 2026, we're drawing down Earth's reserves. The date has been trending earlier every decade since the 1970s. Chile sits in an interesting spot: it's among the world's top producers of copper and lithium, both essential to electric vehicles and renewable energy — the technologies most likely to eventually push Overshoot Day later. But Chilean mining is also one of the heaviest consumers of water in an increasingly drought-stricken Atacama region. The country is literally extracting the materials to solve the crisis while contributing to another dimension of it.

Opinion & Editorial
The Clinic · Left-leaning · July 12

"Empleo como prioridad nacional"

The Clinic editor Pamela Castro argues that Chile is "dangerously getting accustomed" to living with nearly one million unemployed — 38 consecutive months above 8% — and that the political system has normalized a jobs crisis that would qualify as a national emergency anywhere else. The column ran as Congress was celebrating the megarreforma passage, and the contrast is its sharpest point: tax stability for investors got guaranteed in law; job stability for workers got nothing. Castro calls for employment to be elevated to a formal national-priority mandate rather than treated as a residual benefit of favorable fiscal conditions.

Diario Financiero · Right-leaning · July 2026

"Un paso trascendental"

The DF editorial board welcomed the passage of Kast's economic overhaul as the most significant structural shift in Chilean tax and investment policy in a generation. The board argued that reducing corporate tax toward OECD norms and locking in investment conditions will help Chile compete for capital against countries that offer certainty and stable rules — something Chile lost ground on during the previous administration. The editorial acknowledged the Tribunal Constitucional challenge as legitimate process, but expressed confidence the core provisions would survive, and warned against any dilution of the tax-stability clause, which it called the "heart" of the reform's appeal to long-term investors.

Dreampop Shoegaze

Columpios al Suelo — "Noche Estrellada"

Released July 24 on Sello Fisura, "Noche Estrellada" is the first preview from Columpios al Suelo's follow-up to their 2022 album Colores. At eight and a half minutes, it's the most ambitious thing this Santiago band has attempted: overdriven guitar walls layered over a heavy rhythm section, ethereal synths, and mournful vocals circling themes of grief and the persistence of memory after loss. The band describes the approach as "stadium shoegaze" — hymnic in scale, built to envelop. Influences cited by the band include Yo La Tengo and My Bloody Valentine; the extended instrumental passages recall early Slowdive. For fans of Alvvays, Beach House, or Mazzy Star who want more noise and grit in the mix.

Hip Hop 📊 Scene

Movimiento Original Turns 20 — Chilean Hip-Hop's Most Defining Act Marks Two Decades

The group that did more than any other to bring reggaeton and urban hip-hop into the Chilean mainstream is hitting its 20th anniversary. Their arc — from "Soldados del Guetto" (2008) through "Teorema" (2011, widely considered their peak) to "Sobre el Aire" (2023) — traces the full rise of Chilean urban music from street corners to stadiums. A major anniversary lineup has been assembled featuring Ana Tijoux, Portavoz, MC Piri, Seamoon, Soulnastyy, and Spain's SFDK — essentially a roll call of the entire scene that grew around them. The milestone is a useful reason to revisit the back catalog: Teorema still holds up as a complete statement about Chilean street life in the early social-media era.
