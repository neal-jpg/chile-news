Top Headlines
Security · Crime

Kast's "Operación Cancerbero": 700 inmates bused to Bukele-style prison in live-streamed show of force

Chile's government put on its most dramatic crime-fighting spectacle yet: the convoy transfer of roughly 700 high-risk prisoners — including Tren de Aragua gang members and Albanian mafia leaders — to a newly retrofitted maximum-security facility called La Laguna in Talca, 160 miles south of Santiago. President Kast watched from a command post and called it "historic." Footage showed inmates in matching uniforms, hands and feet shackled, loaded onto buses under heavy Gendarmería escort — imagery deliberately echoing El Salvador's President Bukele, whose televised prison crackdowns became global icons of tough-on-crime theater. La Laguna's regime mirrors Bukele's CECOT: mandatory uniforms, strict segregation, heavy surveillance, and restricted family visits — all designed to cut off gang leaders from running operations behind bars, a real problem in Chile's underfunded prisons.

The opposition wasn't buying the production values. Center-left lawmakers called it a "performance" and a "Bukele copycat show," arguing moving prisoners doesn't reduce crime on the street. The INDH — Chile's independent human-rights ombudsman, roughly the equivalent of a US civil-rights commission — announced it would monitor conditions at La Laguna. That triggered a backlash: Republican Party and UDI legislators threatened to push for the INDH's permanent closure if it "protects criminals," prompting INDH director Yerko Ljubetic to warn that the threats themselves were a constitutional crisis — "tomorrow they could do the same to the Contraloría and the Constitutional Tribunal."

Economy

Congress passes Kast's sweeping tax reform — corporate rate drops from 27% to 23%

Chile's Congress gave final approval on August 4 to the Kast government's flagship economic legislation — a package that modifies 36 laws and 15 decrees. The centerpiece: a gradual reduction of the corporate income-tax rate from 27% to 23%, putting Chile closer to the OECD average (think of it as the corporate equivalent of the 2017 Trump tax cuts, but smaller in scale and targeting a different problem). The reform also offers "tax invariability" contracts — multi-decade rate locks similar to US production-tax credits for big infrastructure projects — for periods of 10, 15, or 20 years, aimed at attracting foreign direct investment. Capital-gains exemptions and preferential rates for repatriated offshore money round out the package. Kast called it a plan to "pull Chile out of stagnation"; opponents argue the cuts mostly benefit the wealthy without enough trickle-down.

Economy · Business

Central Bank: Chilean businesses cautious but upbeat as fuel costs bite

The Central Bank's August Business Perceptions Report found Chilean companies in a "moderate" state — not expanding aggressively, not contracting — but still expecting better conditions before year-end. The drag: rising fuel costs tied to US-Iran tensions, filtering into transport and input prices across sectors. GDP grew 2.4% year-on-year in June (driven by mining, services, and retail), but the full-year forecast holds at a modest 1.3%. Inflation is projected around 4% for 2026, with August's monthly figure expected near 0.3% — above prior forecasts. The message: the economy is breathing again after five straight months of contraction, but not yet out of the woods.

Politics · Rights

Ruling party threatens to close Chile's human-rights watchdog over prison monitoring

The row sparked by Operación Cancerbero opened a second front: threats against the INDH (Instituto Nacional de Derechos Humanos). When the rights body said it would inspect conditions at La Laguna, Republican Party and UDI lawmakers fired back, demanding it "stop protecting criminals" and warning they'd push for the agency's permanent closure. INDH director Yerko Ljubetic called the ultimatum a constitutional attack — the same logic, he said, could gut the Contraloría (Chile's independent government auditor, like a souped-up GAO) or even the Constitutional Tribunal. Critics note that Kast's broader 30-bill security package, including a proposed constitutional amendment, may be legally redundant since a Ministry of Public Security created under Boric in 2025 already mandates the same duties.

Astronomy

Chile front-row for a near-total lunar eclipse, August 27–28

Mark your calendar: the night of August 27–28 brings a partial lunar eclipse so deep (96% coverage) it'll look nearly total. Santiago's times — penumbral phase starts 9:23 PM, the dramatic partial bite begins 10:33 PM, maximum eclipse at 12:12 AM. NASA called Chile and South America the best viewing spots on the planet for this one. No telescope, no eye protection required. Find a clear eastern sky by 10 PM and watch the Moon slowly turn reddish-orange as Earth's shadow swallows it. Great outing for kids who are up that late — put it on the calendar now.

Opinion

"Constitutional fireworks": Kast's security overhaul is theater, not substance

Left-leaning · Noticias Los Ríos / Radio Universidad de Chile

A widely-shared column argues that Kast's 30-bill security package — including the proposed constitutional amendment — is legally redundant: Chile's Ministry of Public Security, created by President Boric in January 2025, already mandates exactly what the new reform claims to introduce. The Cancerbero broadcast fits the same pattern: maximum visibility, minimum structural change. The argument isn't that crime isn't a real problem (it is), but that constitutional drama is being used as a substitute for the harder, slower work of judicial and prison reform.

Chileans back Kast's security crackdown — and the polls prove it

Center-right · Infobae / Cadem polling

Polling firm Cadem's latest numbers showed 84% of Chileans support deploying police in the 50 highest-crime neighborhoods — a centerpiece of Kast's security agenda. His overall approval ticked up 2 points to 40% in early August. The argument from center-right commentators: public fear of organized crime is real, Boric-era policies failed to move the needle, and Chileans elected Kast precisely to take dramatic action. The Cancerbero imagery, whatever critics say about optics, is landing well with ordinary Chileans exhausted by years of gang violence in their neighborhoods.

Indie Pop Singer-Songwriter

Francisca Valenzuela — MALDITA

Released July 30 and still dominating Chilean music coverage, Valenzuela's seventh album is generating the biggest critical conversation around a Chilean artist in years. Composed during her first postpartum period, it explores matrescency — the total identity upheaval of becoming a mother — through fiercely dark indie-pop. Rolling Stone called her "a Latin American treasure." El Mostrador noted she "breaks with idealized motherhood to make space for guilt, rage, exhaustion, and unconditional love." The album is unapologetically raw and personal, in the vein of Julieta Venegas's darker territory or Sharon Van Etten. Available everywhere; tour dates include Chile dates through late 2026.

Rock Folk/Folklórico 💿 Vinyl Day recap

Día del Vinilo V — Chile presses its own 7-inches again, celebrates at GAM

Yesterday's fifth annual Vinyl Day at GAM was a milestone for the Chilean music industry: for the first time since the 1980s, 7-inch singles are being pressed domestically, via the new Selknam Record Pressing facility. The free all-day event featured artist signings (Gondwana, Simón Campusano, Congreso, Protistas, Fernando Milagros), panel talks on vinyl manufacturing and graphic design, and roughly 20 new releases — including Los Bunkers' remastered 2003 album La Culpa, a limited Los Prisioneros 7-inch, and new vinyl from De Saloon. A marquee conversation marked the 40th anniversary of Los Prisioneros' debut Pateando Piedras — the record that defined Chilean rock in 1986, roughly the local equivalent of the moment Nevermind had in the US.
