Top Headlines
Economy

Kast's Economic Mega-Reform Clears Congress — Now Faces Vetoes and Court

Chile's Congress completed passage of President José Antonio Kast's sweeping economic reform package on August 4, with the Senate approving the final mixed-committee report 27–22. The so-called "megarreforma" rewrites 36 laws and 15 decrees, with its headline measure being a gradual cut to the corporate income tax rate from 27% to 23% by 2029 — closer to OECD averages. The government called it a signal that "Chile is open for business again."

But the law isn't done yet. The executive will present three presidential vetoes to strip out opposition amendments, and several provisions still face challenges at the Constitutional Court (Chile's equivalent of the Supreme Court reviewing constitutionality). The government wants promulgation completed before September, when the national budget process begins. Critics on the left say the reform disproportionately benefits corporations and wealthier taxpayers while starving municipalities of revenue.

Politics

Pardon Push Explodes: 60 Deputies, Senate Caucus — and a Shocking Senate Showdown

The pressure on President Kast to pardon police and military officers convicted for actions during Chile's 2019 social uprising (the "18-O") has reached a new peak. Sixty ruling-coalition deputies signed a letter to Kast asking him to issue individual pardons, while a new "Caucus for General Pardon" launched in the Senate with members from across the right pushing for a blanket congressional pardon covering all convicted uniformed personnel.

The debate turned explosive on August 5 in the Senate itself. Senator Fabiola Campillai — who was permanently blinded by a teargas canister fired by a Carabinero in 2019 and later won a Senate seat — confronted Senator Camila Flores (RN, one of the pardon advocates) in a heated exchange that escalated to personal insults and physical proximity caught on camera. The Senate's Ethics Commission is now reviewing the incident. Kast has not publicly committed to any pardon, caught between his base's demands and a coalition he needs intact for his economic agenda.

Security · Legislature

Banking Secrecy Commission Set for Aug. 19, as Opposition Presses Crime Agenda

A mixed congressional commission will convene on August 19 to debate lifting Chile's strict banking secrecy rules — allowing financial authorities to access transaction data on suspected criminals without a prior court order, like the US's financial intelligence framework under FinCEN. The tool is seen as essential to tracing drug trafficking money, corruption proceeds, and organized crime financing.

Opposition legislators have criticized the Kast government for omitting the banking secrecy measure from its major security reform package (ACOT), arguing it's one of the most effective instruments to dismantle networks like the Tren de Aragua gang operating in Chile. Senator Juan Luis Castro announced the commission's formation after it was accepted unanimously in committee.

National · Weather

Frontal System Left 2 Dead and Thousands Displaced This Week

A frontal system that moved through central and southern Chile August 2–4 left two people dead, more than 2,000 isolated, and roughly 180,000 without power at its peak. Eleven homes were destroyed and over 1,000 suffered major or minor damage, with the hardest-hit areas concentrated in the Biobío, La Araucanía, Los Ríos, and Maule regions. Red alerts were issued and preventive evacuations carried out in Concepción-area communes.

Conditions have largely stabilized as of Friday, with Santiago seeing clearing skies. Emergency agency SENAPRED continues monitoring affected communities, and recovery aid is being distributed to the estimated 2,476 damnificados (displaced persons).

Astronomy

Mark Your Calendar: A Near-Total Lunar Eclipse Is Coming to Santiago on August 27–28

A partial lunar eclipse on the night of August 27–28 will cover roughly 96% of the Moon's surface — so close to total it will look like a deep-red "blood moon" to the naked eye. Santiago is one of the best-placed cities in the world to see it: the entire event will be visible from start to finish, with the Moon well above the horizon throughout. The partial phase begins around 22:34 local time on August 27, peaking near midnight. Bonus: a comet is also making a close pass by Earth later this month, so August is shaping up to be a decent astronomy month from the Andes.

El Líbero · Right-leaning · Opinion

"¿Indulto general o perdón selectivo?" (General Pardon or Selective Forgiveness?)

El Líbero's editorial column argues Kast should honor his campaign promise to pardon uniformed officers convicted for 18-O-era actions, framing them as having defended democratic institutions against what it characterizes as insurrectionary violence. The column argues that courts applied disproportionate charges to security forces acting under extreme pressure, and that the failure to pardon is demoralizing the police and military. It distinguishes between a blanket "general pardon" (which would require Congress) and individual presidential pardons Kast could issue unilaterally.

CIPER Chile · Investigative / Left-center · Analysis

"Pardons for Officers Convicted of Human Rights Violations Undermine the Rule of Law"

CIPER Chile's investigative team published a detailed analysis arguing that pardoning officers convicted specifically for torture, unlawful killings, and serious bodily harm during 18-O would violate Chile's international treaty obligations — including the American Convention on Human Rights and UN torture conventions — because those conventions prohibit states from extinguishing accountability for documented human rights abuses. The piece notes that unlike ordinary criminal pardons (which are legally uncontroversial), these specific cases were adjudicated as institutional violence, not individual errors.

🌕 Astronomy 📅 Aug 27–28

Near-Total Lunar Eclipse — Plan a Rooftop Night

On the night of August 27–28, Santiago will have a front-row seat to one of this year's best astronomical events: a partial lunar eclipse covering ~96% of the Moon, which will appear dark red and dramatically oversized near the horizon when it begins around 22:34. The entire event is visible from Santiago without any special equipment — just clear skies and a spot away from direct streetlights. A number of observatories in the Andes foothills (including spots near San José de Maipo) typically organize guided viewing nights for events like this.
