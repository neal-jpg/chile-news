Top Headlines
Economy · Trade

Trump's Tariff Hike Blindsides Kast — Even His Own Ministers Are Now Pushing Back

The Trump administration raised tariffs on Chilean exports from 10% to 12.5% last week, effectively overriding the US-Chile Free Trade Agreement that has been in place since 2004. The increase — citing alleged inadequate labor-supply-chain oversight — put Chile in the same bracket as countries with far weaker labor protections, a charge Santiago flatly rejects. The blow landed despite President Kast's months-long diplomatic courtship of Trump: three foreign-minister visits to Washington this year, a deliberate pivot away from China, and Chile's signature on the "Pax Silica" AI supply-chain declaration.

Now even Kast's own cabinet is breaking with protocol. The Clinic reported Tuesday that multiple ministers issued unusually blunt criticisms of the Trump decision — a striking contrast with the administration's earlier posture of studied deference to Washington. Finance Minister Jorge Quiroz acknowledged publicly that the tariff hike "is not positive." The opposition, led by the left bloc, called it a "manifest failure of Chile's foreign policy." Copper, Chile's dominant export, is so far exempt under a separate national-security investigation, but wine, salmon, fresh fruit, and avocados — sectors employing hundreds of thousands of Chileans — take the hit directly.

Economy · Congress

Kast's Economic Megarreform Clears Congress — But a Constitutional Challenge Is Coming

Chile's Congress approved the core of President Kast's sweeping economic overhaul last week, delivering his biggest legislative win so far. The package cuts the corporate tax rate from 27% to 23% over several years, exempts newly built homes from the 19% value-added tax, and allows companies to seek compensation when environmental disputes freeze their investment projects. Finance Minister Quiroz called it "the reform approved in what is essential."

The opposition isn't done fighting: left-bloc lawmakers announced they will appeal to Chile's Constitutional Court (think of it as a cross between the US Supreme Court and a legislative referee) to challenge portions of the law. A Cadem poll showed 56% of Chileans disapprove of the corporate tax reduction specifically. One article remains unresolved in Congress before the full text can be promulgated by the executive.

Economy · Monetary Policy

Central Bank Holds Rate at 4.5% for Seventh Straight Meeting — Middle East Tensions Cited

Chile's Central Bank voted unanimously Monday to keep its benchmark interest rate at 4.5%, the seventh consecutive hold. The decision was widely expected — markets had priced in no change — but the bank's statement flagged fresh headwinds: renewed US-Iran tensions are pushing oil prices higher, the Chilean peso has depreciated, and upside inflation risk has grown. The bank said it will evaluate future cuts "meeting by meeting" with the goal of keeping inflation at its 3% target over a two-year horizon.

Context for American readers: Chile's rate is functionally similar to the US Federal Reserve's federal funds rate. The Fed began cutting rates last year; Chile followed, but the pace has stalled. The Chilean economy contracted 0.5% in Q1 2026 and unemployment rose to 9.4% — the highest since mid-2021 — meaning the bank is trapped between a weak economy that needs lower rates and inflation that makes cutting risky.

Politics · Labor

Unions and Opposition Coordinate a Joint Offensive Against Kast's Labor Agenda

Chile's main labor federation — the CUT (Central Unitaria de Trabajadores), roughly the equivalent of the AFL-CIO — met Tuesday with leaders of the left opposition bloc and the Confech student federation to strategize against what they're calling a government "rollback" of worker rights. Top concern: modifications to the 40-hour workweek law passed under the previous Boric administration. The meeting was described by participants as the most coordinated labor-left alignment since Kast took office in March.

No specific strike dates have been announced, but both the CUT and opposition parties said formal protest mobilizations are not off the table. The Kast government has framed its labor-reform proposals as necessary to improve business competitiveness; unions counter that any weakening of the 40-hour norm would disproportionately hurt lower-income workers who lack bargaining power.

Interesting & Offbeat

Today Is Chile's National Cazuela Day — and There Are Free Tastings Downtown

July 30 is the Día Nacional del Cazuela, a celebration of Chile's quintessential winter stew: a slow-simmered broth of beef or chicken with potato, corn on the cob, pumpkin, and green beans, eaten by virtually every Chilean household throughout the cold months. This year the Centro Histórico de Santiago is hosting public activities and free tastings in plaza spaces around La Moneda, with traditional home cooks and restaurants offering versions of the dish through the afternoon and early evening.

Cazuela is to Chilean winter what chili is to a cold US weekend — deeply comfort-food, deeply regional (every grandmother has the real recipe), and the subject of genuine civic pride. Today's the day to try a proper one if you haven't.

"Trump y Chile: Sonambulismo vs. Realidad"

El Mostrador columnist argues that the Kast government's diplomatic strategy toward Trump was built on wishful thinking rather than analysis: the government assumed that ideological kinship with the Republican right would translate into trade exemptions, when in reality the Trump White House treats Chile the way it treats every country — as a transactional player in an asymmetric power relationship. The column calls the administration's surprise at the tariff hike "sleepwalking" — not bad faith, but a failure to stress-test its own assumptions about how Washington actually works.

Kids 🌿 Outdoors

Teleférico de Santiago & Parquemet — Classic Winter Day Out, Zero Cost to Enter

Parque Metropolitano (Parquemet) on Cerro San Cristóbal is open daily and free to enter on foot. The Teleférico cable car runs year-round from the Pío Nono station in Bellavista up to the Cumbre summit, with panoramic views of the Andes that are especially dramatic on a clear winter afternoon when the mountains are snow-capped. Winter weekdays are pleasantly uncrowded. The Jardín Zoológico is inside the park (separate ticket) and a perennial hit with kids. Check for Día del Niño specials — the zoo has offered free entry for children on August 9 in past years.

Album of the Year
Rock Indie

Los Tres — XCLNT

Released in April 2026, XCLNT is the first album Los Tres has made with their full original lineup in 27 years — Álvaro Henríquez, Ángel Parra, Roberto "Titae" Lindl, and Francisco "Pancho" Molina — and it was recorded at Abbey Road Studios in London. The album's 11 tracks range from grinding rock to vulnerable folk, and the band hasn't softened with age. It was born out of the wildly successful "La Revuelta" reunion tour in 2024, when the group rediscovered that the chemistry was still there. Released through Universal Music Chile, it immediately became the most-discussed Chilean rock record in a generation.

For fans of: classic 90s Chilean rock, Los Bunkers, La Ley, Café Tacvba. Even if you picked it up months ago, it rewards a re-listen on a grey July morning.
