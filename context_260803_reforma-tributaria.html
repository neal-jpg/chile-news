What Is Kast's Economic Reform Package?

Chile's President José Antonio Kast took office in March 2026 promising to reboot an economy that had stagnated under his predecessor. His signature legislative initiative — formally called the Paquete de Reformas Económicas — cleared Congress on July 21, 2026, just five months into his term. One article remains pending before Kast can sign it into law.

What's in it? The centerpiece is a gradual cut in Chile's corporate tax rate from 27% to 23% — bringing it closer to the OECD average. The package also reduces permitting barriers for major investment projects, offers new legal certainty guarantees to foreign investors (think eminent-domain protections and contract stability clauses), and trims some regulatory layers that business groups blamed for slow growth.

American analogy. Think of it as Chile's version of the 2017 Tax Cuts and Jobs Act — a supply-side bet that cutting taxes on business will unlock private investment and create jobs. Like that bill, it passed without the full support of the opposition and over warnings that it would widen inequality and starve social programs of revenue. Finance Minister Jorge Quiroz declared the reform "approved in what is essential."

Why the controversy? Chile's economy contracted 0.5% in Q1 2026 and unemployment climbed to 9.4%. Kast argues the reform is the cure. Critics — including the center-left and left parties that control a significant bloc in Congress — counter that cutting corporate taxes when workers are hurting is the wrong diagnosis. A Cadem poll found 56% of Chileans oppose reducing business taxes.

Why it matters today. Kast needed coalition partners to pass this without a congressional majority — a tricky legislative feat. The win came at a cost: polls show his approval falling to 34% as voters judge results, not intentions. Whether the reform actually boosts investment or widens inequality will be the defining economic question of his four-year term.
