Politics

Kast Tells His Coalition: Security and Jobs First — Everything Else Can Wait

President José Antonio Kast's government sent a clear message this week: the governing coalition's legislative agenda must stay laser-focused on public safety and economic revival, and that means pumping the brakes on bills that fall outside those two priorities. The Secretaría General de la Presidencia — roughly Chile's equivalent of the White House Chief of Staff's office — formally backed Kast's call for coalition parties to hold off on non-priority initiatives.

The move reflects growing impatience inside the administration. Five months into his term, Kast — Chile's most conservative president since the transition to democracy in the late 1980s — has yet to deliver the dramatic crime-reduction numbers he promised voters. Kidnappings linked to the Venezuelan gang Tren de Aragua have doubled in recent years, and public confidence in the security situation remains fragile. The message: stop the internal sideshow and stay on message.

Economy

Trump's 12.5% Tariff on Chilean Exports: Salmon and Wine Take the Hit, Copper Escapes

Washington slapped a 12.5% surcharge on 989 categories of Chilean products — targeting salmon, fruit, wine, dairy, mussels, and wood products — while leaving copper, Chile's most important export, untouched. Finance Minister Jorge Quiroz conceded that the U.S. and Chile are heading in economically "distinct" directions, an unusually frank admission from an ally government. The tariff could cost Chilean exporters an additional US$200 million annually, with the salmon-heavy regions of Los Lagos, Aysén, and Magallanes hit hardest.

Former Finance Minister Felipe Larraín — himself a market liberal — went further on July 31, accusing the U.S. of outright "violating the Free Trade Agreement" with Chile, a pact in force since 2004. The official U.S. justification is that Chile isn't doing enough to combat forced labor. Chile is actively negotiating to be excluded from the list.

Legislation

Tax Megareform Clears Senate in 3 a.m. Vote — One Step from Becoming Law

Kast's flagship tax overhaul passed Chile's Senate on July 16 after a 12-hour marathon session that ended near 3 a.m. The 40-article reform cuts the corporate tax rate from 27% to 23% — think of it as Chile's version of the 2017 U.S. corporate tax cut — and exempts homeowners over 65 from property taxes (contribuciones) on their first home, a popular sweetener. The bill now returns to the Chamber of Deputies for its final review before becoming law.

The session was fractious: the PPD party — a center-left bloc the government had courted as a swing vote — broke ranks at the last moment over how municipalities would be compensated for lost revenue from the 65+ exemption. Opposition senators called the package "a black day for public finances," arguing the rate cuts have no structural revenue replacement.

Security

22 Tren de Aragua Members Arrested — But Chile's Kidnapping Crisis Keeps Growing

Chilean police and prosecutors swept three regions this spring, arresting 22 members of the Venezuelan criminal organization Tren de Aragua and dismantling a network that laundered at least US$13 million through shell companies, converting proceeds from extortion, kidnapping, and drug trafficking into cryptocurrency shipped back to Venezuela. The Kast government has since created a national Kidnapping Observatory to track what officials call a structural shift in organized crime.

Despite the operation, kidnappings attributed to the group have doubled in recent years, and investigators note a worrying shift in victim profile: ordinary merchants and business owners — people with no criminal ties — are now being targeted, not just rival gang members. Think of it as the moment when Chicago's Capone-era gang violence began hitting everyday civilians, not just other bootleggers.

Opinion · Left

"Correr el cerco"

The Clinic · Danilo Herrera · Left-leaning

Herrera argues that the debate around Codelco — Chile's 100%-state-owned copper giant — has quietly shifted rightward: voices inside the governing coalition are now openly floating partial privatization, and the mere fact that it's discussed without shock is itself a concession. The title, "moving the goalposts," is Herrera's metaphor for how the Overton window shifts incrementally until yesterday's extreme becomes today's reasonable proposal. For U.S. context: imagine if a conservative-led Congress started seriously debating selling portions of the Social Security trust fund and no one gasped.

FAMFEST 2026 — International Family Theater Festival (Aug 8–23)

Latin America's biggest family theater festival opens next Saturday, August 8, and runs through August 23 across five venues: GAM, Teatro Mori, Teatro Municipal de Santiago, Teatro UC, and CEINA. Folk / Folklórico

Chola y Gitano — Miel y Hiel

The Valdivia-based duo just won Chile's biggest music prize — the Premio Pulsar 2026 for Best Fusion and Latin American Roots Album — for "Miel y Hiel," a record funded by the national FONDAM music development grant. The album is a multimedia work that weaves music, theater, narrative, and visual elements into a single experience; it draws on southern Chilean folklore — Andean rhythms, coastal folk, the sound of Patagonian rain — filtered through contemporary songwriting. For fans of Violeta Parra's spirit pushed through a modern indie lens, or Natalia Lafourcade for U.S. listeners who've caught her work.
