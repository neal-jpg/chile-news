Politics · Constitutional Court

Chile's top court clips Kast's "Megareform" — but the economic heart survives

Chile's Constitutional Court (TC) on August 14 struck down key provisions of President Kast's sweeping Megareform package, the most ambitious economic legislation of his first five months in office. The justices voided a rule that would have forced the state to compensate companies whose environmental permits were later overturned by courts — a major win for environmentalists — and tightened the scope of a new long-term tax-stability mechanism for large investment projects. Both the government and opposition immediately declared victory: center-left groups celebrated what they could block; the government bloc cheered that the reform's core tax-invariability clause survived intact.

Think of the Megareform like a sweeping deregulation-and-tax-treaty bill rolled into one. The TC ruling is roughly equivalent to the Supreme Court striking down the environmental deregulation piece while leaving the investment-tax provisions standing. Kast also accepted the resignations of two cabinet ministers the same day, adding to a sense of a rocky week for the administration.

Economy

UN body slashes Chile's 2026 growth forecast to 1.6% — one of the region's weakest showings

CEPAL — the UN's economic commission for Latin America, roughly equivalent to a regional CBO crossed with the IMF — released its flagship annual economic study on August 20 and cut Chile's 2026 growth projection to just 1.6%, down from 2.2% forecast earlier in the year. That puts Chile well below the 2.2% regional average and among the least dynamic economies in Latin America. Global trade tensions and domestic structural bottlenecks are cited as the main drags.

A 1.6% growth rate is the macroeconomic equivalent of limping along — technically not a recession, but nowhere near the reactivation Kast promised voters when he won in December. CEPAL projects a partial recovery to 2.5% in 2027 for the region, but Chile's trajectory remains below the continental average for now.

Politics · Polls

Kast stalls at 35% approval; nearly half of Chileans say the country is falling behind

The latest Criteria poll (August 16) puts President Kast's approval at 35%, with 55% disapproving — his numbers have barely moved over three consecutive weeks. More striking is the pessimism index: 47% of Chileans now say the country is moving backward, up sharply from 31% in March when he took office. Only 21% expect improvement in the next six months.

For context: 35% approval after five months is roughly where US presidents tend to sit when their governing coalition starts to fracture — think late-term Bush 43 or Biden in mid-cycle. Kast ran as Chile's hard-right outsider (closer to a MAGA-adjacent candidate than a typical conservative), and the numbers suggest he has held his base but not expanded it.

Consumer Prices · Energy

ENAP projects fuel price hikes this weekend for gasoline, diesel, and LPG

Chile's state oil company ENAP released its weekly reference-price estimate showing increases across the board: 93- and 97-octane gasoline, diesel, and vehicular LPG are all expected to rise when the new price cycle kicks in. Fuel prices in Chile are partly a pass-through from global oil markets and partly managed through the MEPCO stabilization fund — similar to a federal gas-tax buffer — so the swings are somewhat cushioned but not eliminated. Exact cents-per-liter changes should be visible at pumps by Monday.

Astronomy

August's sky over Santiago: a close-pass comet and a lunar eclipse — two for one

August 2026 has been an unusually rich month for skywatchers in Chile. Comet P10/Tempel 2 made its closest approach to Earth in early August and was visible with binoculars from the southern hemisphere before fading. A partial lunar eclipse also falls this month — worth watching from a rooftop or park in Las Condes or Providencia on a clear night. Santiago's winter air, when the smog clears, can be remarkably transparent, and the Andes backdrop makes for an unusually dramatic lunar show.

City · Health Infrastructure

North Santiago gets first budget approval for a new public hospital — 368 beds, $9B CLP

The government approved $9 billion Chilean pesos (roughly $9–10M USD at current rates) this week for land acquisition for a new public hospital in the capital's northern zone — an area that has historically been underserved by public health infrastructure. The facility will eventually have 368 beds and 14 surgical pavilions, serving more than one million residents. Ground hasn't broken, but this budget approval is the first concrete step in a process that's been promised for years.

Radio Universidad de Chile · Aug 20, 2026

"The TC ruling put limits on the Megareform — but it didn't stop the socio-environmental regression"

This left-leaning analysis argues that while the Constitutional Court's partial veto of the Megareform was a genuine win for environmental institutions, the broader damage is already done: enough of the law survived to roll back decade-long safeguards and weaken civil society's ability to challenge permits. The columnist frames it the way a US progressive might frame losing one key EPA case while the entire regulatory agency is being dismantled elsewhere — a partial legal victory that still leaves the playing field tilted toward corporate interests over community protection.

Emol · Aug 19, 2026

"They invent the little frog, they invent everything to avoid approving projects" — Frei on Chile's permitting morass

Former President Eduardo Frei (Christian Democrat, center-left) made headlines with a blunt broadside against Chile's "permisología" — the bureaucratic maze of overlapping permits and observations that critics across the political spectrum say can stall investment projects for years. Speaking at a forum, Frei said agencies "invent the little frog, invent everything to not approve projects" — unusually sharp language from a center-left figure that echoes, on this one issue, Kast's own pro-investment critique. Kids 🔬 Science

Museo Nacional de Historia Natural — "Vertebrados: Anatomía, Evolución, Extinción"

The Natural History Museum in Quinta Normal takes visitors through 400 million years of vertebrate evolution — fossils, articulated skeletal mounts, and hands-on displays about anatomy and extinction. It's an excellent rainy-day (or any-day) option for curious kids and adults. The museum is free on Sundays and low-cost on weekdays. Quinta Normal park itself is one of Santiago's most pleasant winter walking spots, with mature trees and a rowing lake.

🌲 Outdoors

Late-winter Andes views: Cerro San Cristóbal & Teleférico

Late August delivers some of the best Andes panoramas of the year from Cerro San Cristóbal — snowcapped peaks, low winter light, and (on clear afternoons) almost no smog haze. The Teleférico runs weekends and connects Barrio Bellavista to the hilltop. Parquemet's paths are uncrowded mid-week. Bring a layer — temperatures drop fast after 4 p.m. even when midday reaches the low 20s°C. Free to walk up; cable car is a few thousand pesos.
