Top Headlines
Security · Legislature

Kast's sweeping security reform hits a wall — even inside his own coalition

President José Antonio Kast's flagship constitutional reform to create a new "security state of exception" — allowing the president to restrict freedoms for up to 240 days without prior congressional approval to fight organized crime — has stalled badly in the Senate. The government pulled its urgency tag on the bill after Senate President Paulina Núñez, from Kast's own Renovación Nacional party, warned it would not get government votes as written. Kast is now publicly open to changes.

The reform, part of his broader ACOT anti-crime agenda, would let the executive intercept communications and deploy the military to specific neighborhoods without a judge's sign-off. Critics across the spectrum — from former President Boric on the left to libertarians on the right — call it an unprecedented concentration of presidential power. Kast needs a four-sevenths Senate supermajority (about 29 votes) and currently can't count even his own bloc.

Diplomacy

Argentina's Air Force chief claims the Strait of Magellan — Chile files a formal protest

Argentina's Air Force Chief of Staff, Gen. Gustavo Valverde, said in a podcast that "Magallanes, the south, the Drake — all of that is sovereignty" for Argentina. Chile's Foreign Minister Francisco Pérez Mackenna immediately fired back with a formal protest note, calling the strait's ownership "indisputable" under the 1881 and 1984 treaties both countries signed.

Argentina's foreign ministry quickly walked back the general's comments, saying its official position is exactly what those treaties say. But the mayor of Punta Arenas — the Chilean city at the strait's entrance — called the episode "not spontaneous," raising concern about whether Argentina's military and government are on the same page.

Politics · Governance

Kast's state modernization panel excludes the left — fueling bipartisanship doubts

President Kast launched a 120-day commission to redesign the architecture of Chile's government bureaucracy, promising reforms that would "outlast his term." But all 12 members come from the right and center — the Broad Front, Communist Party, and People's Party were left out entirely. Frente Amplio president Gael Yeomans said a table without left-wing input "will not produce proposals that transcend four years." Former President Matthei joined the criticism, urging Kast not to "forget the left."

The episode fits a pattern in Kast's early months: ambitious reform announcements that run into coalition-building problems. Think of it like a congressional committee stacked entirely by Republicans being asked to write bipartisan tax reform — the conclusions are pre-baked, and the opposition won't own the result.

Energy · Infrastructure

Chile's massive north-to-south power line plants its first tower in the Atacama

The Kimal–Lo Aguirre transmission line — Chile's first high-voltage direct current (HVDC) line and the country's largest-ever infrastructure project at $1.5 billion — installed its first major tower in the Atacama Desert this week. The line, 1,346 km from Antofagasta to Pudahuel (just west of Santiago), is designed to carry 3,000 MW of solar and wind power from the north to the capital's grid. Progress stands at about 20%; operational entry is expected in 2029.

This is Chile's answer to a real problem: the north is full of cheap renewable energy but can't pipe it south efficiently, so solar farms often curtail output. Think of it as building a superhighway between a power plant and the city that needs it. Seven thousand jobs are expected during construction across five regions.

Interesting & Offbeat
Curiosity

Argentina and Chile were almost at war in 1978 — over islands, not the strait

The flare-up over the Strait of Magellan this week prompted many Chileans to dust off a piece of history that's not widely known outside the region: in 1978, Argentina and Chile came within hours of full-scale war over the Beagle Channel islands, with Argentine jets reportedly fueled and ready. Pope John Paul II personally intervened, and the Vatican-mediated Treaty of Peace and Friendship followed in 1984 — the same one Argentina's Air Force chief seemed to forget this week. The Punta Arenas mayor's comment that the episode was "not spontaneous" is a direct nod to this history.

A columnist at BioBioChile argues that the ACOT security reform's core provision — a state of exception lasting up to 240 days with no judicial check — is a constitutional Trojan horse. The real goal, the columnist writes, isn't defeating gangs but normalizing emergency presidential powers. Libertad y Desarrollo · Right · Think-tank Opinion

"The other security reform: why institutions matter more than emergency powers"

Chile's leading right-wing think-tank argues that the ACOT debate is missing the forest for the trees. The real security gap isn't constitutional powers — it's prosecutorial capacity, prisons, and border enforcement. The column is broadly sympathetic to Kast's anti-crime goals but argues the constitutional reform as drafted is a political liability that distracts from the more effective (if less dramatic) institutional fixes that don't require supermajorities to pass.

Kids 🎭 Theater & Circus

FAMFEST 2026 — 33 family shows at GAM, Teatro Mori & more

FAMFEST is a citywide family performing-arts festival running across multiple venues — GAM (Centro Arte Alameda / Lastarria), Teatro Mori, Teatro Municipal de Santiago, Teatro UC, and CEINA. The lineup spans 33 shows: theater, circus, marionettes, and live music, organized by age group from sensory shows for toddlers to full productions for teens. It's one of the few festivals that takes kids' programming seriously as an art form rather than an afterthought. Check each venue directly for specific show dates and tickets.

Kids 🔬 Science

MIM (Museo Interactivo Mirador) — ongoing · La Granja

The Museo Interactivo Mirador in La Granja is Santiago's best science museum for kids — hands-on exhibits covering earthquakes, bubbles, astronomy, and energy that kids can actually touch and operate. Good for a half-day. Admission runs about $8,500 for adults and $7,500 for kids 2–12. It's not flashy, but it's reliably excellent for school-age children, especially on a winter Saturday when outdoor plans get rained out. Located at Av. Punta Arenas 6711.

Sistemas Inestables — Vestigium

A progressive electronic album from the Chilean trio that layers synthesizers with raw brass instruments and heavy drums — an unusual combination that lands somewhere between Klaus Schulze and a Mingus ensemble. Vestigium is a serious listen, not background music.
