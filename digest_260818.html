Politics · Congress

Kast's Security Reform Stalls — His Own Coalition Won't Give Him the Votes

President José Antonio Kast's flagship constitutional security reform is stuck — blocked, for now, by his own allies. This week the government trimmed its proposal from 11 constitutional amendments down to 6 or 7, trying to win over wavering senators. It's not enough. Kast needs 29 Senate votes — a 4/7 "supermajority" (think a U.S. constitutional amendment requiring two-thirds of the Senate). His coalition holds only 26. UDI party chief Guillermo Ramírez says rewriting the constitution to fight crime "damages institutions." Renovación Nacional president Andrea Balladares supports the security goals but rejects changing the constitution to deliver them. Without centrist opposition votes, the reform cannot pass.

The proposal would enshrine public security as an explicit state duty, allow a national registry of organized crime members, and create a new "state of exception" giving authorities expanded powers in gang-controlled zones — a tool Chile tested partially in the northern border region during migration-related violence. The next few weeks will test whether Kast can broker a deal or must shelve the centerpiece of his security agenda before the spring legislative window opens.

Politics · Public Opinion

Kast Stuck at 35% Approval as Chilean Gloom Deepens

The latest Criteria survey finds President Kast's approval frozen at 35%, with disapproval climbing to 55% — roughly one in three Chileans backs him. The gloomier number: Chileans who believe the country is moving backward jumped from 31% in March to 47% today, while those who think it's advancing fell from 28% to 20%. Hope as the dominant national emotion has fallen from 53% in February to 36%; sadness has more than tripled from 5% to 17%. Security spending keeps Kast's floor firm, but economic anxiety — cost of living, wages, hospital waiting lists — is eroding the national mood.

Immigration

Chile's Deportation Pace Has Nearly Doubled Under Kast

Chile executed 1,330 deportation orders in the first seven months of 2026 — almost double the 753 carried out in the same period last year. Irregular border crossings fell 33% over the same stretch. Think ICE under Trump, but scaled for Chile's smaller population: a centralized enforcement push backed by political will from the top. Rights groups warn that Chile's detention infrastructure is not built to sustain this pace humanely. The government says the decline in irregular crossings proves deterrence is working; it has become one of Kast's most visible early wins with his base.

Foreign Policy

Chile Quietly Drifts Toward Trump's 'Donroe Doctrine' Orbit

The U.S. State Department this month released a video formalizing the "Donroe Doctrine" — Trump's reboot of the Monroe Doctrine, declaring U.S. primacy across the entire Western Hemisphere. Chile has attended two meetings of the "Shield of the Americas" — a club of right-aligned Latin American governments aligned with Washington — without formally signing on. The Kast government has described the U.S. as Chile's "principal strategic partner," but almost no public debate has happened about what full adherence would mean: curtailing Chinese trade and investment (China is Chile's largest trading partner), military alignment with Washington, and participation in U.S.-led regional security architectures.

Interesting & Offbeat

Kast Personally Watched as 191 High-Risk Prisoners Were Shipped South — 'Bukele-Style'

Last Thursday, President Kast monitored via video link from La Moneda as 191 high-risk inmates — including nine organized-crime gang leaders — were transferred by road from Santiago prisons to the new fortified La Laguna facility in Talca, 250 km south. He called it "a historic day." The operation, dubbed "Operación Cancerbero," was explicitly modeled on El Salvador President Nayib Bukele's mass-imprisonment crackdown — which gutted gang violence there but drew serious human rights warnings. Ministers of Interior, Justice, and Security attended in person at the Talca end. Critics from the previous Boric government called it political theater; the Kast administration says it's the first step in wresting back state control of prisons from gang networks.

El Mostrador · Left-leaning · Aug 15, 2026

"La Odisea: Chile en el mar de la doctrina Donroe"

An El Mostrador columnist argues Chile is sleepwalking into the U.S. Donroe Doctrine orbit without any meaningful public debate. The government has joined two "Shield of the Americas" meetings — a U.S.-organized grouping of right-aligned governments — but the implications (curtailing China ties, military realignment with Washington) haven't been debated in Congress or civil society. The column draws on Homer's Odyssey: Chile is being swept by a current it doesn't fully understand, toward a destination it hasn't consciously chosen. Left-leaning outlet; the argument maps roughly to Democrats' concerns about U.S. vassalage in smaller allies' domestic debates.

The Clinic · Reporting on right-wing institutionalism · Aug 17, 2026

"La Constitución no es un árbol de Pascua" — The Right's Own Dissenters

The Clinic reports on a crystallizing critique inside Kast's own coalition: that using the constitution as a vehicle for security policy is a mistake. UDI members warn that loading the constitution with specific operational measures — a registry of crime gang members, new emergency states — sets a bad precedent and weakens the document's institutional authority. The critique echoes a classically conservative argument: the constitution should set structure, not solve the crisis of the month. If Kast gets his amendments today, what stops the next government from adding its own? Note: right-leaning editorial voices this week are focused internally on this institutional debate rather than producing external commentary.

XV Festival del Día Mundial del Folklore — Free, Plaza de la Cultura, Sat Aug 29

The 15th annual World Folklore Day festival takes place in the plaza in front of La Moneda (the presidential palace) on August 29. Free, all-ages, featuring folk dance and music from Chile, Peru, Brazil, Colombia, and other Latin American countries. Think Andean flutes, criollo cueca, Afro-Brazilian percussion — a good way to introduce kids to the region's musical diversity in a well-situated public square. Santiago doesn't do outdoor folk festivals often; this is worth making time for.

Princesa Alba — Tus estaciones

Chile's Princesa Alba dropped her most personal single yet this week: "Tus estaciones" (Your Seasons), a melodic indie-pop track built on notes she kept between 2016 and 2017 while commuting on the Santiago Metro as a student. The song has a warm 2000s Latin-pop nostalgia — Belanova, Supernova, Kudai — with a brief cameo from Javiera Parra. The music video, directed by Esteban Vargas Roa and shot in the Metro, layers memory fragments with retro tech in a quietly cinematic way. One of the more fully realized Chilean indie releases in a while. For fans of: Javiera Mena, early Camila Moreno, Julieta Venegas.
