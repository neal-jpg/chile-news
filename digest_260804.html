Top Headlines
Politics

Senate Votes Today on the Final Piece of Kast's Economic Overhaul

Chile's Senate convenes this Tuesday for what should be the last legislative hurdle before President José Antonio Kast signs his flagship economic legislation into law. The "Proyecto de Reconstrucción Nacional y Desarrollo Económico y Social" — known informally as the megarreforma — passed the Senate in July after a 12-hour overnight session, but one article got kicked to a joint House-Senate committee: how to compensate municipalities for the property-tax revenue they'll lose once the reform exempts seniors' first homes from property taxes. The committee resolved the dispute, and today senators vote on that resolution.

Even after this vote, the story isn't quite over. The government announced it will send Congress three separate vetoes to strip out provisions it didn't want in the final bill — a ban on compound interest (anatocismo), a rule giving debtors a "financial right to be forgotten" after five years, and a mandate forcing companies to pay small businesses within 30 days. The opposition has also filed challenges at Chile's Constitutional Tribunal (roughly equivalent to the US Supreme Court) against other parts of the bill. The reform's core — cutting corporate taxes from 27% to 23%, reinstating full tax integration, and extending invariability guarantees to investors — should survive regardless.

Economy & Tech

Chile's Paper Banking Grid Cards Are Gone — Here's What Replaced Them

As of August 1, the tarjeta de coordenadas — a physical grid card Chileans used for decades to authorize online bank transfers by looking up a row-and-column code — is officially retired. Chile's financial regulator (the CMF, which functions like a combined SEC and FDIC) mandated a switch to two-factor authentication: banks now require something you know plus something you have or are. BancoEstado uses BE Pass (a PIN) or BE Face (facial scan). Banco de Chile uses Mi Pass. Most banks now use app notifications or SMS codes.

The change was delayed once already because of concerns that older customers would be left behind. A narrow exception remains: Santander is still offering grid cards to seniors, customers with incompatible phones, and people with disabilities. If you haven't updated your banking app, today is a good moment.

Security

Government Reports 17% Drop in Homicides, Touts 1,000+ Deportations

President Kast chaired a security committee Monday (Aug 3) and released preliminary data showing a 17% year-on-year decline in homicides, along with a 45% drop in kidnappings reported to the PDI (Chile's investigative police, analogous to the FBI) and a 60% surge in drug seizures. The government also highlighted that deportations of undocumented foreign nationals have now surpassed 1,000 for the year — a milestone it frames as evidence that its strict immigration enforcement posture is working.

Opposition politicians and criminologists urge caution: the data covers only part of the year and doesn't account for reporting shifts. Organized crime networks — particularly Venezuelan and Albanian groups — remain active in extortion and drug trafficking, especially in the northern regions near the Bolivian border. The government plans additional urgency legislation on security before September.

Economy

Growth Is Sluggish but Quiroz Insists the Second Half Will Be Different

The OECD cut Chile's 2026 GDP growth forecast to roughly 1.7%, and several consecutive months of IMACEC (Chile's monthly economic activity index, like a smaller version of US quarterly GDP) showed contractions. Unemployment ticked up 0.5 percentage points over the past year, even as employment is technically growing — because the labor force is expanding faster, as more Chileans re-enter the job market. Finance Minister Jorge Quiroz maintains that the megarreforma and a pipeline of mining investment projects will make the second half of 2026 "very dynamic."

The Central Bank projects inflation settling around 3% through Q3. Consumer prices have been pushed up partly by fuel costs tied to Middle East tensions, which feed into transportation and logistics throughout the economy.

Culture

In Chile, August Is Basically Christmas — But for Kids

Chile celebrates Día del Niño (Children's Day) on the second Sunday of August, which this year falls on August 9. The effect: all of August becomes an unofficial Month of the Child, with retailers, restaurants, theme parks, and cultural venues competing hard for family audiences. Disney On Ice is at Movistar Arena. PAW Patrol Live rolls in August 23. FAMFEST international family theater festival runs all month across multiple venues. Free art workshops for kids are happening at MAVI UC every Saturday. The Ñuñoa municipality is activating 18 neighborhood plazas with free programming this weekend (Aug 8–9).

For context: Chile doesn't have a December school break (the school year ends in December), so August is one of the only times families get a collective cultural moment mid-year. The commercial calendar has learned to lean into it.

Education

Kast Rebrands Education Ministry Around Reading and Math Basics

Education Minister Arzola launched "Chile Aprende y Avanza" in July — a program that aims to turn the Ministry of Education from a regulator into a "support and facilitator" for schools, with a specific focus on strengthening reading and math from the earliest grades. The pitch mirrors elements of the "back to basics" movement seen in some US states: less ideology, more foundational skills. Chilean standardized scores in both subjects have lagged OECD averages for years.

Opinion & Editorial
The Clinic · Left-leaning · Jaime Mañalich

"Viejos; pero útiles"

Former health minister Mañalich (a Piñera-era center-right official who has become a regular voice in the left-leaning Clinic) argues that Chile's political class treats its elderly as a constituency to appease with tax breaks rather than a population deserving of genuine dignity and care infrastructure. He supports the property-tax exemption for seniors in the megarreforma, but warns it is a thin gesture if not backed by expanded home-care services and a reformed pension system. Chile's older population is growing faster than its caregiving capacity — a crisis he says neither left nor right is taking seriously enough.

El Líbero · Right-leaning · Alejandro San Francisco

"El Congreso Estratégico Ideológico del Frente Amplio"

Historian and conservative commentator Alejandro San Francisco analyzed the Frente Amplio's first Ideological Strategic Congress, held in late July. His read: if the FA (Chile's left-wing coalition, analogous to a Bernie Sanders–style progressive bloc) returns to power in 2030, it has formally committed to a "socialist, feminist, and ecologist" platform rather than the more centrist language it used in 2021. San Francisco frames this as a healthy clarification — voters will know exactly what they're getting — but warns that the left's organizational discipline puts the Chilean right on notice to sharpen its own ideological identity before the next election cycle.

Folk/Folklórico Indie

Cualmarcelo — Calendarios (debut album)

Cualmarcelo is a Temuco-based artist (the Lake District, 670 km south of Santiago — a region with strong Mapuche cultural roots and a very different soundscape than the capital). The debut album Calendarios has been on digital platforms since April, but got its first physical CD presentation in Santiago at Matucana 100 on July 30, bringing it to Metropolitan Region audiences for the first time. The album sits in a folk-indie space that draws on southern Chilean textures without being explicitly traditional — quieter and more atmospheric than the noisy Hotel Records compilation above. Worth exploring if you like finding regional voices that haven't been smoothed out for the Santiago market.

For fans of: Nano Stern's earlier work, quiet Chilean folk that doesn't feel like a museum exhibit.
