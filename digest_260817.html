Politics · Constitutional

Steinert Faces Investigators Tonight — Impeachment Clock Expires Tomorrow

Former Security Minister Trinidad Steinert testifies before the Chamber of Deputies investigative commission at 7:30 PM tonight, with the opposition's constitutional-accusation deadline expiring Tuesday morning. Chile's Contraloría General — the country's independent federal auditor, roughly a hybrid of the US GAO and an Inspector General — ruled in July that Steinert illegally ordered the PDI (Chile's FBI equivalent) to share confidential files from an organized-crime investigation into the "clan Chen." She also violated conflict-of-interest rules by making that request about a case she had personally worked as a regional prosecutor in Tarapacá before entering government.

An acusación constitucional — Chile's version of a congressional impeachment of a cabinet member, not the president — requires a majority vote in the Chamber and then two-thirds in the Senate to be upheld. Success strips the official from public office for five years. The commission was only formed after the opposition threatened to go straight to the AC; Kast publicly praised Steinert even after the Contraloría ruling, feeding left arguments that La Moneda knew about the overreach and shielded her. Tonight's testimony may determine whether the opposition believes an AC is winnable before the window closes.

Economy · Legislation

Kast's Megarreforma Is Fully Law — What Actually Made It Through

Chile's landmark economic reform completed its journey last week when the Senate approved Kast's vetoes, clearing the final hurdle. The core package now law: corporate income tax drops from 27% to 23% over four years, approaching the OECD average; home purchases are exempt from VAT; and large investors receive a multi-year guarantee that their tax rules won't change. Three amendments the opposition had inserted during legislative debate were stripped via presidential veto: a ban on compound interest (anatocismo), a "financial forgetting" provision that would have let Chileans reset their credit histories, and a 30-day payment requirement for small-business suppliers.

Tech · Infrastructure

Google's "Alisios" Cable Will Connect Chile to the Americas' Digital Backbone

Google announced "Alisios," a submarine fiber-optic cable linking Chile to Panama and the Dominican Republic, targeted for operation by 2028. The US$11.5M project — 99% Google-funded, 1% by state company Desarrollo País — is part of Google's "Americas Connect" initiative. Chile already hosts the Humboldt cable to Asia-Pacific; Alisios adds a high-speed transatlantic Americas corridor. Better latency and network resilience for Chilean internet users, and a signal of foreign investment confidence the Kast government was quick to celebrate.

Mark August 27: Santiago Gets a Near-Total Lunar Eclipse

Santiago sits in prime viewing territory for a partial lunar eclipse on the night of August 27–28 that will cover 96% of the moon — as close to total as you can get without crossing the line. The partial phase begins at 10:33 PM; peak (deep copper-red) at 12:12 AM; ends at 1:52 AM. No equipment needed — just clear skies and a view of the east. It's a Thursday night, so it runs late, but Santiago's cerros and flat rooftop terraces make for spectacular positions. Circle it now.

Right · Economy

La megarreforma que sobrevivió a la oposición — y lo que importa

El Líbero / Ex-Ante — center-right

Center-right analysis frames the presidential veto as a correction, not a defeat: the three amendments removed — the compound-interest ban, credit-history reset, and 30-day SME payment rule — would have introduced retroactive legal uncertainty into a reform designed to project stability. El Líbero's line: the corporate-tax reduction to near-OECD levels and the tax-stability guarantee for large investments send the right signal after years of investor hesitation under Boric. The unresolved question it sidesteps: whether tax relief concentrated at the top of the capital structure trickles down in the wage growth and employment the government is promising.

Rock Indie

Los Tres — XCLNT (Apr 2026)

Still the biggest Chilean music story of the year and still worth the deep-listen. Los Tres reunited their original 1990s lineup — Álvaro Henríquez, Roberto Lindl, Francisco Molina, Miguel Tapia — for the first time in 27 years and recorded at Abbey Road in London. The 11-track result spans taut guitar rock ("Cantar y amar," "Como llegaste te vas") and quieter devastation ("Alma a la deriva"). Critics called it "raw but sophisticated" — the band's signature complex rhythms and textured guitars, recorded with high-fidelity warmth that serves the legacy. For fans of: Wilco, Vetiver, or anyone who wants classic Chilean rock with the production it always deserved.
