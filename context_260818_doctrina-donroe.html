What Is the Donroe Doctrine?

The Donroe Doctrine is President Donald Trump's 21st-century update of the Monroe Doctrine — the 1823 U.S. foreign policy principle that declared the Western Hemisphere off-limits to European powers. Trump's version, formalized in a State Department video released in August 2026, extends that logic into the present: the U.S. claims primacy over all political and economic arrangements in the Americas, and expects hemisphere-wide governments to align with Washington rather than Beijing, Moscow, or other rivals.

The name blends "Monroe" with "Don" — a nod to Trump's brand — and is sometimes called the "Monroe-Trump Doctrine" in international press. It echoes the original Monroe Doctrine's imperial undertone: the U.S. as the region's senior authority, not an equal partner among nations.

How it works in Chile: The practical vehicle is a grouping called the Shield of the Americas — regular meetings of right-aligned Latin American governments convened under U.S. auspices. Chile, under President Kast, has attended two such meetings since March 2026 without formally joining. The government has not publicly committed to the full framework, but it has described the U.S. as Chile's "principal strategic partner," signaling sympathies.

American analogy: Think of it as the U.S. proposing a version of NATO — but for the whole hemisphere, with the U.S. setting the agenda — and asking Chile to sign up. Full membership would likely require curtailing major Chinese infrastructure and trade deals (Chile's largest trading partner is China), limiting arms purchases from non-U.S. suppliers, and coordinating security policy with Washington rather than through multilateral bodies like the OAS or CELAC.

Why it matters today: Chile's participation in Shield of the Americas meetings has happened quietly, without congressional debate or broad public discussion. Critics say Chile is drifting into a U.S.-designed architecture that constrains foreign policy autonomy — the same autonomy Chile has exercised for decades by trading freely with China, Europe, and the U.S. simultaneously. Proponents argue that aligning with Washington is a pragmatic response to a world reorganizing into blocs.
