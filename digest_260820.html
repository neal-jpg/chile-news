Emergency · North Chile

Mudslide Devastates Tocopilla; Kast Declares State of Catastrophe

A violent flash flood (aluvión) tore through Tocopilla — a coastal desert city of 25,000 in the Antofagasta region — on Tuesday, killing at least one person, leaving three missing, and displacing more than 2,000 residents. Mud flows reportedly reached speeds of 90 km/h with a destructive force estimated at 7 tons; about 6,000 customers lost electricity and all road access into the city was cut. President Kast flew to Antofagasta to oversee the emergency response and signed a constitutional state of catastrophe for the Tocopilla province — a designation that unlocks rapid mobilization of military logistics and public funds, roughly analogous to a US presidential major disaster declaration.

Tocopilla is also the hometown of soccer star Alexis Sánchez (currently at CF Montreal), who posted a message to Kast on social media: "I trust in you, President — if you need help, I am available." The message crossed partisan lines and generated a small social-media storm, with some fans accusing Sánchez of being politically "amarillo" (a Chilean slang insult meaning spineless or a sell-out). Sánchez shot back: "Faithful to my style."

Politics · Security

Kast's Security Reform Hits Senate — and His Own Coalition

President Kast submitted a sweeping constitutional security reform to the Senate on urgent terms, giving Congress just 15 working days to act. The centerpiece is a new "public security state of exception" — allowing the president to suspend civil liberties in areas controlled by organized crime for up to 120 days, extendable to 240 days without a congressional vote. Think of it as a domestic version of martial law that the president could invoke without needing to go back to Congress for months at a time.

The trouble: even Kast's own libertarian coalition partners are revolting. Senator Evelyn Matthei–wing libertarians, including Senator Kaiser, called the proposal "the closest thing to an increasingly undemocratic system" and said it doesn't solve the underlying security problem. The opposition rejects it flatly. A CIPER Chile analysis warned the measure rests on "the hypothesis of internal war." The government insists extraordinary powers are needed; critics say they're unconstitutional overreach from a president who campaigned on small government.

Economy

Tax Reform Sits at Constitutional Court Before Taking Effect

Chile's landmark National Reconstruction Law — passed by Congress in early August after weeks of debate — is now before the Constitutional Tribunal (TC), Chile's equivalent of the US Supreme Court's power of judicial review, after opponents challenged several provisions. The reform's centerpiece is cutting the corporate income tax rate to 23% and creating a tax stability regime that lets large investors lock in their tax rate for 10–20 years in exchange for a small premium. The government designed it to reactivate an economy that saw growth forecasts slashed to roughly 1% for 2026 after a rough first half.

The TC review is expected to resolve within weeks. If it clears — or clears with minor modifications — the law moves to enactment and publication, and the new corporate rates begin phasing in. Economists are watching closely: the reform is the government's biggest economic bet, and any partial invalidation could complicate the investment-certainty signal Kast has been trying to send to foreign capital markets.

Tech & Business

Chile Now Has Only One Apple Store Chain — After Regulator Waves Through Merger

Chile's National Economic Prosecutor's Office (FNE — think the US FTC) green-lit MacOnline's acquisition of its main rival, Aufbau, for the equivalent of about $10.5 million. Both are Apple Premium Resellers — the authorized store chains that sell Macs, iPhones, and accessories with official service centers. The FNE concluded their stores have little geographic overlap in most cities, so competition won't be substantially reduced. MacOnline is owned by Sonda, Chile's largest IT company. For Apple users in Chile, it means a single chain now handles all "official" repairs and service — handy to know if your MacBook needs a fix.

Opinion & Editorial
El Mostrador · Left-leaning

"Rebellion on the Right: Kast's Security Reform Can't Even Get His Own Votes"

El Mostrador's political desk argues that the government's constitutional security reform has exposed a fundamental contradiction at the heart of Kast's coalition: a libertarian base elected to shrink the state is now being asked to approve the most expansive presidential security powers since the dictatorship era. With coalition senators already signaling they may vote against it, and the opposition uniformly opposed, the reform faces an uphill path — and a self-inflicted one. The piece suggests the government mistook electoral momentum for ideological permission to go further than its own voters bargained for.

Libertad y Desarrollo · Right-leaning think tank

"Public Security: The Other Reform Chile Needs"

Chile's leading right-of-center think tank makes the case that the security constitutional reform is the necessary complement to the economic agenda: investment climate improvements are meaningless if crime and organized violence remain unchecked. The piece argues that critics who invoke civil liberties risks are ignoring the concrete daily harm of cartel activity in northern and southern Chile, and that a focused, time-limited state of exception — with congressional oversight after 240 days — is a proportionate tool, not an authoritarian one. Chile's current constitutional framework, they argue, is simply not equipped to deal with 21st-century transnational crime networks.

New Releases
Indie Alt-Rock

Silabario — New Album (Sello Fisura)

Santiago indie-rock quartet Silabario drops their second studio album on independent label Sello Fisura, produced by Víctor Muñoz (known for work with Niños del Cerro and Asia Menor). The record goes heavier and noisier than their 2018 debut "Lo Malo Va a Pasar," leaning into 90s alternative — think early Radiohead static crossed with Chilean post-punk grit. Lead single "Pienso en ti como un lugar" sets a template of nine songs about resistance, love, and collectivity. For fans of: Niños del Cerro, Astro, La Hell Yeah.

Tenemos Explosivos — Victoria

One of Chile's sharpest political rock bands returned in 2026 with "Victoria," their third album and first new material in seven years — coming after the vinyl reissue celebration of debut "Derrumbe y Celebración." Tenemos Explosivos play a confrontational guitar-rock with a clear political message; the new record continues that tradition with what early coverage describes as their most focused songwriting yet.
