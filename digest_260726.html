Trade / Economy

Trump Raises Chile's Tariff to 12.5% — Salmon, Wine, and Fruit at Risk

The Trump administration imposed a 12.5% tariff on Chilean exports effective July 24 — up from the previous 10% baseline — citing inadequate legal protections against forced labor in Chilean supply chains. Washington investigated 60 countries under the same framework; Chile is among those penalized. Copper, Chile's dominant export to the U.S. and the backbone of the bilateral trade relationship, is explicitly exempt. But salmon, wine, fresh fruit, and timber products are all hit, with the agricultural lobby warning the measure affects more than 50% of Chilean shipments to the American market.

The National Agriculture Society (SNA) flagged "significant competitive damage and uncertainty for thousands of workers and producers." Finance Minister Jorge Quiroz acknowledged that Trump's economic direction is "distinct" from Chile's but stopped short of a formal diplomatic response. Former foreign minister Heraldo Muñoz wrote that Chile should "negotiate firmly and without being cozy" with Washington, arguing its copper and lithium leverage gives it real cards to play.

Economy / Congress

Kast's Megarreforma Is Nearly Law — Opposition Heads to Constitutional Court

President Kast's sweeping economic overhaul cleared the Chamber of Deputies on July 21, his government's biggest legislative win to date. The package cuts the corporate income tax from 27% to 23% over several years — moving Chile toward the OECD average — and adds housing incentives, investment-stability clauses guaranteeing fixed tax conditions for large projects over 20 years, and property-tax exemptions for elderly homeowners on their primary residence.

One article — compensation to municipalities for lost property-tax revenue — remains in a joint Senate-House committee, delaying full promulgation. The opposition has announced it will challenge parts of the reform at Chile's Constitutional Court (roughly the equivalent of challenging a law before the U.S. Supreme Court). A Cadem poll shows 56% of Chileans oppose the corporate tax cut even as the economy shrank 0.5% in Q1 2026 and unemployment stands at 9.4%.

Climate / Emergency

New Flooding Alert in Maule — Chile's Storm Recovery Is Not Over

A Red Alert was declared Friday for Curicó and Molina in the Maule Region after river overflow risk. Chile is still nursing wounds from the record mid-July storm system — beginning July 15 — that killed 13 people, isolated roughly 60,000, and destroyed or severely damaged more than 2,800 homes across ten regions. Santiago alone received 101mm of rain over 48 hours on July 16–17, the second-highest two-day July total in 110 years of records.

The Kast government's National Reconstruction Law — a 40-provision mega-bill covering emergency housing funds, infrastructure repair authorization, and spending flexibility — is almost fully enacted after passing both chambers. The accompanying "Plan Chile se Levanta" (Chile Rises) provides VAT deferrals and loan relief for affected families. Military personnel have been deployed in Coquimbo and the Huasco province, where the state of catastrophe remains in force.

Security

Homicides Down 13%, Border Crossings Down 86% — Kast's Security Numbers Move His Way

Security Minister Martín Arrau presented first-semester data showing homicides down 13% (442 victims vs. 508 in the same 2025 period), violent robberies down 16%, and unauthorized border crossings at the three main northern checkpoints down 86.1%. Crime reduction was Kast's defining campaign promise, and the numbers are trending in his direction. His approval rating has rebounded to roughly 54% (AtlasIntel/Bloomberg, early July), up from its low point earlier in 2026.

The caveat: drug and weapons-related crimes rose during the same period. The minister acknowledged Chile still has "a long road ahead" to return to crime levels of a decade ago. Improvements were especially notable in the Metropolitan Region, Valparaíso, and Biobío; Tarapacá, Coquimbo, and Maule moved in the wrong direction. The government also announced it would seek urgency for legislation to strengthen protections for Carabineros officers.

Left / Center-Left · Tariffs

"Chile Must Negotiate With Firmness — Not Coziness — With Trump"

Heraldo Muñoz · Radio y Diario Universidad de Chile · Former Foreign Minister · Left-leaning

Writing in response to the new 12.5% tariff, Chile's former foreign minister argues that Kast's instinct toward ideological alignment with the Trump administration is a strategic mistake. Chile is not just any trading partner — its copper and lithium sit at the heart of the global energy transition and the U.S. tech supply chain. Muñoz calls for a firm, public posture in coalition with Brazil and Mexico rather than quiet diplomacy, warning that "making yourself agreeable" to Trump rarely yields better terms and risks sacrificing Chile's long-term interests for short-term political optics.

Right / Center-Right · Megarreforma

"The Reform Is Chile's Best Bet — Opposing It Is Defending a Failing Status Quo"

El Líbero · Right-leaning · Economic commentary

El Líbero editorialists argue that Chile's 27% corporate tax rate has deterred investment for nearly a decade, and with Q1 GDP contracting and unemployment at a five-year high, the status quo is not a neutral option — it has a cost measured in jobs not created and projects that went elsewhere. The reform brings Chile closer to the OECD average and adds 20-year stability clauses that give large investors the predictability they need. Critics of the tax cut, they write, conflate who nominally pays the tax with the separate question of whether the investment ever materializes under the old regime.

Santiago Rocks 2026 Confirms Lineup — A Classic Rock en Español Bill

The festival dropped its lineup July 22–23 and it's a serious nostalgia bill: Los Jaivas, Illya Kuryaki and the Valderramas, Los Auténticos Decadentes, Joe Vasconcellos, Vilma Palma e Vampiros, and 2 Minutos — all on one stage at Hipódromo Chile (Independencia) on December 13. Los Jaivas are the headliner to know: one of the most important bands in Chilean history, blending Andean folk instruments, prog rock structures, and 1970s psychedelia into something no one else has replicated. Illya Kuryaki (Argentine duo) bring Flea-meets-Latin-hip-hop energy to a bill that otherwise leans classic rock. For fans of: Lollapalooza throwbacks, classic rock en español, anyone curious about the music that shaped Latin America from the 1970s through the 1990s. Tickets on sale via PuntoTicket.
