Economy

Unemployment Hits 9.4% — Worst in Five Years, Even as Economy Posts First Growth in Months

Chile's jobless rate climbed to 9.4% in the April–June quarter, its highest level in five years, with seven of Chile's 16 regions now above 10% unemployment. The grim labor figures arrive alongside a rare piece of good news: the monthly IMACEC economic activity index (Chile's version of a US monthly GDP readout) posted its first year-on-year gain in six months — up 2.4% in June, double the median forecast. The gap tells the story of a lopsided recovery driven by the mining sector rather than the job-rich industries — construction, retail, services — where most Chileans work.

Finance Minister Jorge Quiroz is holding to his 2026 growth target of 1.8%, and the government's landmark Mega-Reform (passed August 4) promises corporate tax cuts and investment incentives designed to rekindle hiring. But with the reform's key provisions facing presidential vetoes and a Constitutional Court review, meaningful job relief remains a 2027-at-earliest proposition for most workers.

Property & Local Government

Property Tax Fight Erupts: Are Kast's Exemptions a Gift to Santiago's Richest Communes?

Buried inside last week's National Reconstruction Law was a high-profile measure: a 100% exemption on "contribuciones" — Chile's property tax, like a US real estate tax bill — for all homeowners 65 and older, costing municipalities an estimated $220M per year. Now a second fight has broken out over fiscal valuations. The government's own data shows roughly three-quarters of seniors already pay zero contributions because their property's assessed value falls below the tax threshold — meaning the new exemption mainly benefits wealthier owners whose homes are actually assessed high enough to owe something.

A bloc of 16 opposition mayors has rejected the government's compensation formula, calling it a subsidy for already-wealthy communes at the expense of poorer ones. Meanwhile, the SII's (Chile's IRS) property valuation system — which hasn't kept pace with real-estate markets — is now also under fire: in some of Santiago's richest streets, fiscal valuations are so far below market price that the owner of a multi-million-dollar home owes nothing.

National

Second Storm in Three Weeks: 2 Dead, 180,000 Without Power Across Southern Chile

A second major frontal system struck center and southern Chile around August 2, killing two people in Panguipulli (Los Ríos region) and cutting power to 180,000 customers — just as communities were beginning to recover from July's catastrophic floods, which killed 13 people and may cost up to $700M to repair. Red alerts were active across Maule, Biobío, La Araucanía, and Los Lagos; more than 6,000 people were isolated and nearly 400 homes sustained major damage.

The back-to-back disasters arrive as the government's new National Reconstruction Law — only just cleared by Congress on August 4 — is still being organized. Programs to help storm-affected families exist on paper; the administrative machinery to disburse them is being assembled in real time.

Bonus trivia: a 2024 labor-law reform officially moved the Día del Niño public holiday to the second Sunday of October for employment purposes — but most families, schools, and venues have kept celebrating on the second Sunday of August, the traditional date. Kids win either way; employers have to calculate twice.

Journalist Wins Miss Chile on a Bold Abortion Answer — and Social Media Splits

Dominga López, a 24-year-old journalist from Viña del Mar, was crowned Miss Universo Chile 2026 on August 2 — and the moment that clinched it was her live answer about Chile's contentious "Escucha su Corazón" bill (a conservative-backed proposal to restrict abortion access). Her clearly pro-choice response drew sustained applause in the auditorium. López will represent Chile at the 75th Miss Universe in San Juan, Puerto Rico on November 24.

In a country actively debating abortion policy and women's public roles, a beauty queen making an unambiguous political statement landed differently than the usual pageant diplomacy. Kast voters and conservative commentators went to X; feminist accounts and left-leaning media celebrated. The Clínica's headline: "The Miss Chile who wasn't afraid."

"Security Is Also Built From City Hall"

An op-ed in El Mostrador argues that municipal governments — not just the national Interior Ministry — are a critical layer in Chile's crime-prevention strategy. The author profiles one commune's "Más Seguridad, Más Comunidad" program — 58 officers, targeted patrols, a 24-hour communications center, $1.3B CLP investment — which saw crime cases fall 7.1%, robberies 4.8%, and violent robberies drop 17.7%. The piece is an implicit counter-argument to Kast's top-down national security plan (ACOT) announced last week: federal packages work better when local governments already hold the ground. Think of it as arguing for community policing as the foundation for any big federal security law.

"Class War? The Real Debate Behind the Property Tax Fight"

An El Mostrador column frames the contribuciones exemption controversy as a proxy for a deeper class conflict: the mega-reform's property tax relief disproportionately benefits wealthy homeowners in already-well-funded communes, while poorer municipalities absorb the revenue shortfall. The author calls it "the right relief with the wrong formula" — the real structural problem is that Chile's property valuation system hasn't tracked real-estate markets in years, so many of the richest owners already pay zero. Any exemption layered on top of a broken system amplifies inequality. Right-center columnists at El Líbero and Ex-Ante focused this week on investor legal certainty post-reform, with no single stand-out piece for today.

Indie Pop

Francisca Valenzuela — MALDITA

Released July 30, Francisca Valenzuela's seventh album is her most uncompromising work yet. MALDITA ("Damned One") is a 12-track reckoning with motherhood, postpartum, rage, and tenderness, recorded on her own Frantastic Records label. Rolling Stone called it "ferociously dark and uncompromising," concluding that Valenzuela is "one of the most generous artists of her generation — and a Latin American treasure." Think Mon Laferte's confrontational intensity applied to the experience of a Chilean woman artist navigating new motherhood. She's touring Chile, Spain, the US, and Mexico behind it.

Los Tres — XCLNT

April's biggest Chilean release is still reverberating. Los Tres — one of Chile's defining bands of the 1990s — returned with their full original four-piece lineup for the first time in 26 years, recording XCLNT at Abbey Road Studios in London. Think brooding, literary Chilean rock in the vein of a melancholic alt-rock tradition — for fans of Nick Cave's storytelling applied to Santiago's streets.
