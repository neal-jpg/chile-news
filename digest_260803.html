Top Headlines
Emergency

Chile's Second Winter Storm in Three Weeks Kills 2, Displaces 6,000+

A powerful frontal system struck Chile's center-south over the weekend, leaving two people dead — a couple killed when a tree fell on their van on a road in the Los Ríos Region — and over 6,183 people displaced from their homes. More than 2,000 additional people were isolated by flooding and road closures, and 180,000 lost electricity at peak impact. Red alerts were activated in Biobío, Ñuble, La Araucanía, Los Ríos, and Coquimbo.

This follows the July 14–21 storm that killed 13 people and left more than 3,000 displaced — making this Chile's worst back-to-back winter emergency season in recent memory. La Araucanía alone accounts for more than 5,000 of the displaced. The government deployed military units to guard evacuated homes against looting and has opened emergency shelters throughout affected communes.

Politics

Kast's Approval Falls to 34% — Lowest Yet — as Economy and Security Drag

A new Agenda Criteria poll released Sunday puts President Kast's approval at 34%, down two points from last month and down six points since June — his lowest reading since taking office in March. Disapproval stands at 55%. The slide tracks three converging pain points: the economy contracted 0.5% in the first quarter (Kast inherited a weak recovery), unemployment climbed to 9.4%, and promised security improvements haven't yet materialized in the crime statistics.

Political scientists at Universidad de Santiago note that Chileans largely expected faster results on crime — a top campaign promise — and are growing impatient five months in. Think of it like a new US president who wins on "law and order" messaging but whose first-year FBI crime stats look unchanged. The economic reform package passing Congress (see below) is Kast's main legislative win, but voters tend to judge outcomes, not legislation.

Economy

Congress Passes Kast's Landmark Tax Reform — One Article Still Pending Signature

Chile's Congress approved the bulk of President Kast's sweeping economic overhaul on July 21, handing him his biggest legislative win yet. The package — think of it as Chile's version of the 2017 Tax Cuts and Jobs Act — gradually cuts the corporate tax rate from 27% to 23%, adds investment-certainty guarantees for foreign companies, and reduces regulatory barriers to major infrastructure and mining projects. Finance Minister Jorge Quiroz called it "approved in what is essential."

One disputed article remains before the package is ready for promulgation; that vote is expected in the coming days. The opposition — center-left and left parties — voted largely against it, arguing the tax cuts primarily benefit large corporations and will starve schools and hospitals of revenue. A Cadem poll found 56% of Chileans oppose reducing business taxes. Kast needed coalition partners to clear the votes despite his Republican Party not holding a congressional majority.

Politics · Economy

Codelco Privatization Debate Erupts Within Kast's Own Coalition

Last week's most combustible policy fight came from inside the governing coalition. Republican Senator Arturo Squella — who leads Kast's own party — proposed selling off 15–25% of Codelco, Chile's state-owned copper giant, to bring "private oxygen" to a company he called strangled by bureaucracy. Think of Codelco as the Chilean equivalent of a federally-owned ExxonMobil that funds roughly 10% of the national budget; even Pinochet's military dictatorship kept it state-owned.

The Kast government quickly distanced itself, saying privatization "is not in the plans," though it remained open to selling non-core subsidiaries. But then the small Libertarian Party one-upped Squella entirely: sell Codelco outright and hand every Chilean a check for up to $2 million pesos (about $2,000 USD). The opposition's response: "Not even during the dictatorship." The debate has scrambled traditional alignments and forced Kast to navigate between his reform mandate and property-rights absolutists on his own right flank.

Offbeat · Politics

Libertarian MP Offers Every Chilean a $2,000 Check — If They Agree to Sell Codelco

Johannes Kaiser, the flamboyant libertarian congressman who has been compared to a Chilean Ron Paul by way of Javier Milei, made his most theatrical policy pitch yet: privatize all of Codelco — the world's largest copper miner — and deposit up to 2 million pesos (roughly $2,000 USD) into every Chilean's bank account. He called it "capitalismo popular" and framed it as finally giving Chileans a direct stake in the copper riches beneath their feet. Critics pointed out the math doesn't quite work (Codelco's assets divided by 20 million people is considerably more complicated than a check) and that trading a perpetual revenue stream for a one-time payout would be like selling your house for a year's rent.

Opinion
The Clinic · Left-leaning · Danilo Herrera · August 1

"Correr el cerco" — Moving the Goalposts on Chile's Untouchables

Herrera argues that the Codelco privatization debate is less about copper economics than about a deliberate strategy of expanding what's politically thinkable. By proposing to sell what even Pinochet kept public, Senator Squella and the Libertarian Party are "moving the fence" — normalizing conversations that shift the center of Chilean political gravity rightward. The column warns that once an idea enters serious legislative debate, it stops being extreme: today's fringe proposal becomes tomorrow's negotiating position.

El Líbero · Right-leaning · Francisca Echeverría · August 1

"The Tax Reform Debate Revealed a Left With No Alternative Blueprint"

Echeverría argues that the center-left's defeat on the economic reform package exposed a deeper problem: opposition parties proved adept at critiquing Kast's supply-side vision but offered no coherent counter-proposal for generating investment and fixing Chile's fiscal deficit. "The right wanted investor certainty. The left offered global criticism of the economic model rather than an alternative fiscal architecture." She contends that until the opposition builds a positive program — not just a veto — it will keep losing the legislative battles that shape the next four years.

FAMFEST 2026 — Latin America's Largest Family Theater Festival (Aug 8–23)

The 19th edition of FAMFEST runs August 8–23 across Santiago and reaches Concepción, La Pintana, Lampa, and San Ramón. Over 33 performances of theater, circus, dance, puppetry, and live music — 21 of them with Chilean Sign Language interpretation. Age-grouped programming runs from early childhood through adolescence. Main venues: GAM, Teatro Mori Bellavista, Teatro Mori Recoleta, Teatro Municipal de Santiago, Teatro Camilo Henríquez.

Opening night (FREE) — Saturday August 8: "Fauna: una amistad incondicional" by OtroArte at GAM's Central Plaza — aerial dance, live music, acrobatics, and giant puppets. No tickets needed. This is a genuinely excellent way to introduce kids to live performance in a relaxed outdoor setting.

Rock Indie

Los Tres — XCLNT

Released April 10, 2026 on Universal Music Chile, XCLNT is the first studio album from Los Tres' original four-piece lineup (Álvaro Henríquez, Roberto Lindl, Ángel Parra, Francisco Molina) in 26 years — recorded at Abbey Road's Studio 2 in London. The 11-track, 39-minute set has been submitted for Latin Grammy consideration, which would be a first for the band's most celebrated creative era. The lead single "Cantar y Amar" leans into the band's signature mix of Chilean folk melody with hard rock punch. For fans of: any era of Los Tres, Caifanes, classic Argentine rock.
