Economy / Legislature

Chile's Landmark Economic Overhaul Is One Committee Vote From Becoming Law

Chile's Congress voted Tuesday to approve nearly all of President Kast's sweeping "megarreforma" — formally the Law of Reconstruction and Economic and Social Development — handing him the biggest legislative win of his four months in office. The reform cuts corporate income tax from 27% to 23% by 2029 (approaching the OECD average), exempts new home purchases from VAT, and locks in a 20-year tax stability clause for investments exceeding $350 million. One clause — a municipal revenue compensation formula — remains in a mixed House-Senate committee and is expected to resolve this week, after which Kast signs it into law.

The backdrop is grim: Chile's GDP contracted 0.5% in Q1 2026, unemployment has hit a five-year high of 9.4%, and new polling shows 43% of Chileans expect economic conditions to worsen in the second half of the year — up from 36%, the most pessimistic reading since 2015. The IMF cut its Chile growth forecast to 1.8% for 2026. Kast argues the reform will rebuild investor confidence and create jobs. The opposition calls it a gift to the wealthy that risks thinning the safety net for social programs.

Trade / US Relations

Trump Hits Chile With a 12.5% Tariff — On a Country It Has a Free Trade Deal With

The Trump administration on July 23 imposed an additional 12.5% tariff on Chilean exports, part of a broad action against 60 countries the White House accuses of "insufficient efforts to combat forced labor." The move is diplomatically awkward: Chile and the US have had a Free Trade Agreement since 2004, and Kast's government has cultivated ideological alignment with Trump on crime, immigration, and market economics. Chile's trade ministry rejected the legal basis of the measure and is pushing for product-by-product exemptions.

Critically, copper is excluded — shielding Chile's largest export sector. The industries most exposed: salmon (Chile is the world's second-largest producer after Norway) and fresh fruit. The contradiction is hard to miss: Washington is slapping a tariff on a country whose copper and lithium sit at the heart of the US tech and clean-energy supply chain, under a free trade framework both governments have cited as a model. Think of it as the US penalizing Canada on a forced-labor technicality while separately lobbying Ottawa for military cooperation.

Politics / Legislature

Kast's Own Party Fired a Congressional Missile at the Opposition — and Hit Itself

Kast's Partido Republicano and their Libertarian allies filed a constitutional accusation in June against former center-left Finance Minister Nicolás Grau, claiming irregularities in the outgoing government's final Public Finances Report. A constitutional accusation is Chile's version of a congressional impeachment motion — if approved by both chambers, it can bar the target from public office for five years (think a Senate censure vote with real teeth). The Partido Republicano saw it as a way to politically wound the left; instead, it detonated inside the governing coalition.

Chile Vamos partners — the more pragmatic right-wing parties UDI and Renovación Nacional (think mainstream Republicans vs. MAGA) — refused to support the accusation, calling the legal case too weak. The Senate rejected all four chapters. The defeat exposed the coalition's deepest fault line: Kast's hardline base vs. the traditional right that has to work across the aisle to pass anything. Kast personally intervened to calm a very public family fight. The episode is now a reference point for why the right's 2026 legislative calendar has been rockier than its margins suggest it should be.

Art / Culture

A Giant Wave of Migration Stories Just Washed Into Matucana 100 — and It Moves

Chilean visual artist Patricio Vogel opened "La ola" (The Wave) this Saturday at Matucana 100's main gallery — his most ambitious show yet, the product of a decade of research. The centerpiece: enormous translucent fabric panels that billow and shift continuously, driven by a ventilation system, while a theremin-based soundscape composed with Agnes Paz weaves instrumentals with recorded testimonies from migrants about memory and belonging. Twenty-three mechanical flip-panel displays — like the old departure boards at Santiago's Alameda station — cycle through a phrase by Haitian anthropologist Michel-Rolph Trouillot on how past and present speak to each other. Free; open through September 13. Wed–Fri noon–2pm and 3–9pm; Sat 4–9pm; Sun 4–8pm. Av. Matucana 100, Barrio Yungay.

Left / Center-Left · Political Strategy

"The New Opportunity for Chile's Center and Left — But Only If They Stop Playing Defense"

El Mostrador · Left-leaning · July 14

A columnist in El Mostrador argues that Chile's center-left has made a strategic error: playing the right's game on the right's chosen terrain — crime, immigration, fiscal discipline — while ceding the framing of every major debate to Kast's government. The left's strongest ground is precisely where Kast is weakest: housing costs remain high, public health is underfunded, and the megarreforma's trickle-down benefits won't appear in any paycheck before the next election. The argument borrows from US political science: whoever sets the agenda wins, and the left has spent eight months reacting. The moment Kast's economy visibly underdelivers — and that moment may be arriving — the opposition needs an agenda ready, not just a critique.

Right / Center-Right · Trade

"The Tariff on Chile Is Trump's Own Contradiction — We Should Name It That, Publicly"

Forbes Chile · Center-right · July 23

Forbes Chile's coverage of the tariff frames Chile as a victim of US internal politics rather than any genuine trade dispute. Chile built its economic model around the rules-based international order — free trade agreements, OECD norms, transparent investment frameworks — and has cooperated closely with Washington on critical minerals, democracy promotion, and regional security. To then be lumped with 59 other countries on a "forced labor" pretext that none of Chile's actual export sectors engage in is, from a center-right perspective, a betrayal of the very economic partnership Chile went out of its way to build. Rather than seeking quiet diplomatic exemptions that tacitly validate the charge, Chile should name the contradiction loudly, at the WTO and in bilateral channels — and use it to negotiate from strength, not embarrassment.

Rock Prog Indie

Hesse Kassel — Morir Saltando

Released July 15, this Santiago six-piece's second album landed like a depth charge in the Chilean scene: within a week, Rate Your Music users had voted it the best album of 2026 so far. "Morir Saltando" (Dying Jumping) was recorded with all six members playing simultaneously in the studio — a deliberate choice to capture the energy of a live set rather than construct it in post. The result is visceral: jagged, muscular guitars that shift from noise rock to jazz-inflected prog within a single track, all anchored by haunted lead vocals reviewers have compared to Slint or early Failure. Chilean rock rarely breaks through onto international music-nerd sites, but this one has.

Standout track: "Pornomisteria," described as "an avant-prog funhouse that throws you into so many explosive grooves." Recorded in Santiago, available on Bandcamp and streaming. For fans of Slint, Don Caballero, Mogwai, or 90s post-rock in general.

Dreampop

Alodia — Duloxetina (single)

Santiago dreampop project Alodia returned this month with "Duloxetina" — a single that borrows the name of a common antidepressant as a metaphor for emotional suppression: functioning for the world rather than for yourself. Opens delicate and atmospheric, all reverb-washed guitars and soft synths, then builds to a cathartic break of crashing drums and distorted guitar — the emotional release the verses have been denying. Alodia has been one of Santiago's quieter but more consistent dreampop acts since 2017, and "Duloxetina" suggests a third album is in progress. For fans of Beach House, Alvvays, or fellow Chileans Coloresantos.

📊 Charts

Chile's Charts: Urbano Rules the Mainstream, But the Independent Scene Is Its Own World

Chile's Spotify chart is dominated by reggaeton and urbano — regional Latin pop, trap, and dembow, largely from Colombia, Puerto Rico, and Chile's own urban acts. The releases featured in this tab sit completely outside that commercial ecosystem. The Chilean independent scene runs on its own circuit: Bandcamp, the Rockaxis platform and magazine, Radio Sónica, Super 45, and a network of small festivals and cultural centers. When Chilean rock or dreampop artists describe themselves as "outside the mainstream," they mean exactly that — a separate world, not a mild divergence from the pop charts. Worth knowing the context when looking at any release coverage from outlets like Rockaxis or Futuro Chile.
