Top Headlines
Politics · Security

Kast's Security Mega-Reform Stalls — Even His Own Coalition Won't Vote for It

President Kast's flagship ACOT anti-crime package — eight constitutional amendments submitted to the Senate on August 17 — has hit an immediate wall: the government cannot even count on all 26 senators in its own coalition, let alone the 29 votes needed to pass a constitutional reform. The centerpiece is a new "estado de excepción constitucional de seguridad pública" (constitutional state of exception for public security) that would allow Kast to restrict movement, authorize wiretaps, and suspend certain civil rights for up to 240 days without prior congressional approval.

Senate president Paulina Núñez (Renovación Nacional, a key coalition party) warned bluntly that the state-of-exception clause will not get votes. Former minister Álvaro Elizalde called the draft "una chambonada" — a botched job. On Tuesday, Kast signaled he is open to changes, likely stripping the most controversial provisions. Constitutional lawyer Javier Couso called the original proposal "substantially dangerous for democracy" — giving the president what he described as near-omnipotent emergency powers without legislative checks. Think of it as a proposed executive-branch expansion that even Republican-equivalent senators in Kast's own caucus opposed.

Economy · Mining

Copper Holds at Historic Highs, Cushioning Chile's Finances

Copper has been running at record prices through August — it hit US$6.59 per pound mid-month and the 2026 year-to-date average is now US$6.00/lb, up nearly 40% versus the same point in 2025. Demand from AI data-center buildout, EV manufacturing, and power-grid expansion is outpacing supply from aging mines with declining ore grades. Chile supplies roughly a quarter of the world's copper, so a sustained price rally fills the national treasury and supports the peso.

Think of it like US natural-gas windfalls during an energy crisis — a commodity tailwind that boosts government revenues without requiring legislation. The IPSA (Chile's main stock index, roughly its S&P 500 equivalent) rose alongside copper. Caveat: economists warn the rally is partly driven by importers stockpiling ahead of potential US tariffs on copper, which could unwind sharply if trade tensions ease.

Economy · Policy

Kast's Big Tax Cut Is Law — But the Constitutional Court Has Final Say

Congress passed the National Reconstruction Plan on August 4, Kast's sweeping tax reform cutting Chile's corporate rate from 27% to 23% by 2029 and returning the system to full dividend-tax integration (eliminating double-taxation). About 150,000 companies — representing 90% of private investment — fall under the new rules. Small businesses get a transitional 12.5% rate through 2027.

Opposition legislators immediately filed an unconstitutionality challenge — the Chilean equivalent of a law being held up by the Supreme Court after Congress passes it. The Constitutional Tribunal must rule before it can take effect. The government calls it the engine of economic recovery; critics argue it disproportionately benefits large corporations and hollows out social-spending capacity.

Northern Santiago to Get a Major New Public Hospital — 368 Beds, 14 Surgical Suites

The Health Ministry announced on August 18 the construction of a significant new public hospital for Santiago's northern zone, serving a catchment of more than one million residents. Northern Santiago comunas — Quilicura, Renca, Pudahuel — have long been underserved in health infrastructure compared to wealthier eastern comunas like Las Condes and Providencia, making this a meaningful equity investment if construction proceeds on schedule.

Interesting & Offbeat

Chilecompra Turns 23 — The Little Government Website That Moves Billions

Today marks 23 years since Chile launched Chilecompra (formally Mercado Público), its online public procurement platform, in 2003. What started as a transparency measure has grown into one of Latin America's most sophisticated government-purchasing systems, handling tens of billions of dollars in public tenders annually — from hospital supplies to road contracts. Chile's Contraloría General (roughly its GAO) uses it as a first-stop audit tool. For reference: it's what US Federal procurement looks like on SAM.gov, but arguably more unified and better adopted at the local-government level.

"El Gobierno improvisó su reforma y la democracia paga el precio"

El Mostrador · Editorial · Center-left

El Mostrador's editorial desk argues Kast's ACOT constitutional reform is a case study in poor legislative craftsmanship: the executive rushed a sweeping state-of-exception clause into a constitutional bill without building consensus even within its own coalition. The piece warns that the powers in the original draft — restricting movement and authorizing wiretaps for up to 240 days without prior Senate approval — recall the emergency-decree culture of Chile's authoritarian period, an uncomfortable parallel in a country with a living memory of Pinochet. The column calls for the government to step back and negotiate a genuinely bipartisan security bill rather than treating constitutional reform as a fast-track shortcut.

Ex-Ante · Column · Center-right

Ex-Ante's commentary acknowledges the ACOT reform's tactical failures but argues that abandoning the substance would be a grave mistake: Chile faces a genuine organized-crime threat, and the window for meaningful constitutional reform narrows with every month of the legislative calendar. The column urges the government to table the most constitutionally contentious elements — specifically the 240-day state of exception without prior congressional approval — while pushing hard for the remaining package. Doing nothing is not an option when criminal networks have become entrenched in neighborhoods from Antofagasta to Santiago's periphery.

Rock Indie

Los Tres — Inri (single from XCLNT)

The original four-piece — Álvaro Henríquez, Roberto Lindl, Ángel Parra, Francisco Molina — released their reunion album XCLNT in April after 26 years apart, recorded at Abbey Road's Studio 2, and it remains the story of Chilean rock's year. "Inri" is the latest single drawn from that record: a stripped-back, folk-tinged track that shows the band's range beyond the album's harder cuts. XCLNT's eleven songs feel fresh and unmistakably Los Tres, landing somewhere between La Sangre en el Cuerpo and Hágalo Usted Mismo. For fans of: Pixies' quieter moments, Caifanes, cueca brava.

Dreampop Indie

Candelabro — Candelabro Canta a Los Prisioneros

One of the more intriguing Chilean projects of the year: ascending indie-dreampop act Candelabro reinterpreted Los Prisioneros classics through their signature hazy, reverb-saturated sound. Think of Los Prisioneros as Chile's equivalent of The Clash or The Smiths in terms of cultural weight — sharp political anthems from the 1980s — filtered here through gauzy shoegaze production. Two tracks debuted in March; physical 7-inch editions released at Día del Vinilo at GAM (August 15). The contrast between the pointed originals and the soft-focus arrangements is genuinely compelling. For fans of: Beach House, Galaxie 500, early Cocteau Twins.
