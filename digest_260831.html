Politics · International

Chile hosts global far-right summit this week — and it's splitting Kast's coalition

Santiago will be the venue September 3–4 for the V Regional Meeting of Foro Madrid, an international network of far-right parties co-founded by Spain's Vox in 2020. Confirmed attendees include Argentine President Javier Milei, Italy's Giorgia Meloni, Vox leader Santiago Abascal, and members of the Bolsonaro family — all converging on the Centro de Eventos San Carlos de Apoquindo. Kast, one of the original signatories of the "Madrid Charter," will attend as host and head of state.

The event is fracturing Kast's ruling alliance. His coalition partners at RN (Renovación Nacional — think moderate establishment Republicans) have quietly distanced themselves and will not attend as a party. The center-left opposition called it a "summit of leaders who despise democracy," while the Communist Party argued Kast must present himself as president of all Chileans, not a partisan activist. Think of it as if a U.S. president invited a MAGA International summit to Washington and his own party's senators refused to show up.

Security · Congress

Kast's sweeping security reform stalls — even his own coalition balks at new emergency powers

Kast's flagship constitutional security reform — part of the ACOT anti-crime agenda — is under a 15-day fast-track deadline in the Senate, submitted with "suma urgencia" (the Chilean equivalent of a Congressional emergency fast-track). Its most controversial piece: a brand-new "estado de excepción de seguridad pública" that would let the president suspend civil liberties — freedom of movement, assembly, and private communications — for up to 120 days to fight organized crime.

The Senate's own president, Paulina Núñez of RN (Kast's coalition partner), warned flatly that the state-of-exception clause cannot pass without her party's votes and called it a bridge too far. Sociologist Juan Pablo Luna told The Clinic the proposal is "confirmatory of ideological fanaticism" and a sign of the "bukelization" of the government — borrowing the model of El Salvador's Nayib Bukele, who suspended civil liberties en masse to fight gangs but drew wide international criticism. The opposition is calling on Kast to withdraw the entire reform.

Economy

Chile sidesteps technical recession — but the all-clear is narrow and growth is weak

The IMACEC (Chile's monthly economic activity index — like a monthly GDP readout the US doesn't have) grew 2.4% in June compared to a year earlier, breaking a five-month streak of contractions and technically keeping Chile out of a recession. A "technical recession" in Chile, as in the US, means two consecutive quarters of negative GDP; that was the threat after January–May all came in negative.

But the relief is thin. The full-year growth forecast stands at just 1–1.75% for 2026, far below what Kast promised on the campaign trail. Low non-mining tax revenues are keeping the Finance Ministry on edge, and analysts warn that the economy is still fragile. The mining sector (primarily copper) is carrying the load; the domestic consumer economy is only tentatively recovering.

Interesting / Offbeat

Chile's Atacama Desert is becoming the Southern Hemisphere's window on the most violent events in the universe

Construction is now underway at Paranal (the same Atacama ridge that hosts ESO's Very Large Telescope) on the CTAO — the Cherenkov Telescope Array Observatory — which will be the world's largest gamma-ray observatory. Gamma rays are the most energetic form of light, produced by things like supernova remnants, black-hole jets, and dark-matter annihilation. There's no equivalent Southern Hemisphere facility anywhere on Earth yet. First telescopes are slated to be deployed by the end of 2026, and 10% of observing time is reserved for Chilean scientists. A second complementary facility, the SWGO, is also planned for the Atacama Astronomical Park. Chile is quietly becoming the planet's astronomy capital.

Opinion · Right-leaning

El Líbero · Richard Kouyoumdjian · August 26, 2026

"¿Puede el modelo Bukele funcionar en Chile?"

El Líbero columnist Richard Kouyoumdjian takes the opposite tack, examining El Salvador's Bukele security model — the mass detentions, suspended rights, and dramatic crime-rate drop — and asking whether a version of that approach could work in Chile given rising organized crime. He argues that critics who dismiss it on civil-liberties grounds are not grappling honestly with the real costs of inaction. El Líbero is Chile's leading right-libertarian outlet and has generally been sympathetic to hard-line security measures.

🎭 Theater

GAM — Tres Marías y una Rosa · From September 3

One of the essential texts of Chilean theater returns to the stage at GAM: a 1979 play about four women working at a flower factory who organize a labor strike, written during the Pinochet years when saying these things directly was dangerous. Think of it as a Chilean Norma Rae — feminist labor drama with deep roots in the national consciousness. A fitting opener for the spring season. GAM is at Av. GAM — La Pérgola de las Flores · Ongoing

La Pérgola de las Flores is the closest thing Chile has to its own West Side Story — a beloved musical from 1960 set in a Santiago flower market, full of chilenismos and classic songs every Chilean knows by heart. 🏛️ History

Museo de la Memoria — Los niños del Winnipeg · September 3–4

The Museo de la Memoria y los Derechos Humanos presents Los niños del Winnipeg — the story of 2,000 Spanish Civil War refugees, many of them children, who came to Chile in 1939 aboard the SS Winnipeg, a boat chartered by Pablo Neruda at the request of Chile's Popular Front government. It's a powerful piece of Chile's own immigrant history and a reminder that Chile once opened its doors widely. Located at Matucana 501 in Quinta Normal, metro Quinta Normal.

Rock Indie

Los Tres — XCLNT

Still the essential Chilean album of the year, four months after its April 10 release: Los Tres returned with their original lineup after 27 years away, recording at Abbey Road with a 11-track set that has held up to repeated listening. The videoclip for "Como llegaste te vas" was produced by Punkrobot — the Santiago animation studio that won the Oscar for Historia de un Oso. Los Tres are to Chilean rock what Wilco are to American indie rock — foundational, generational, and completely their own. If you haven't put this on yet, rainy Monday morning is the moment.
