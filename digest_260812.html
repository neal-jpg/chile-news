Security

Kast Sends Congress a Constitutional Anti-Crime Package — Including New "Emergency Powers" for Gang-Controlled Zones

President Kast submitted Chile's most sweeping security legislation since democracy was restored in 1990: the Agenda Contra el Crimen Organizado y el Terrorismo (ACOT). The centerpiece is a proposed constitutional amendment creating a brand-new presidential power — a "security state of exception" that could be declared in any territory effectively controlled by a criminal organization, allowing the military to deploy alongside police, restrict movement, and fast-track asset seizures. The package also bundles 28 bills covering new crimes, a national blacklist of criminal organizations, harsher sentences for cartel leaders, and separate maximum-security prison wings.

Kast asked Congress to act with maximum urgency, framing the reform as essential to reclaim neighborhoods where gangs have displaced the state. Critics warn the new powers could be used to militarize poor communities under the guise of anti-crime enforcement. Think a constitutional RICO Act crossed with targeted martial-law authority — written directly into the national charter.

Politics

Kast's Own Coalition Is Pressuring Him to Pardon Officers Convicted for 2019 Protest Abuses

Sixty lawmakers from Kast's governing coalition — spanning his Republican Party, UDI, RN, Evópoli, the Libertarian Party, and PDG — sent the president a formal letter requesting pardons for 13 Carabineros and military officers convicted for abuses during Chile's 2019 mass uprising. A bipartisan group of senators then went further, forming a "Bancada por el Indulto General" (pardon caucus) pushing for sweeping clemency for the entire security apparatus involved.

Kast has resisted, insisting he'll review each case individually rather than issue blanket pardons. The standoff is creating real tension within his coalition. The analogy for US readers: imagine Republicans pressuring Trump to pardon National Guard members convicted of civil rights violations during a protest crackdown — except these convictions involved documented abuses during a national crisis that left hundreds blinded and dozens dead.

Weather Emergency

New Storm Hits Northern Chile — Already Reeling from July's Deadly Floods

A new round of extreme precipitation is hitting northern Chile today, with 60–80mm possible in the Andean foothills between Arica and Coquimbo. The government declared a preventive climate emergency for five northern regions from August 10–16, activating pre-positioned resources ahead of the storm. The north never fully recovered from a catastrophic July 20 event that killed at least five people, isolated roughly 100,000, and triggered a formal state of catastrophe declaration for the Coquimbo region and Atacama's Huasco province.

For context: northern Chile is one of the driest places on earth — this is like a tropical storm hitting Death Valley. The Atacama desert averages less than 1mm of rain per year in places; an event dumping the equivalent of a month's rain in an hour overwhelms any drainage infrastructure.

Economy

Senate Clears the Last Hurdle on Kast's Tax Overhaul — Legal Battles Still Ahead

The Chilean Senate voted 27–22 on August 4 to approve the final contested article of Kast's sweeping megarreforma económica — a package that cuts corporate taxes from 27% to 23%, the biggest business-friendly tax shift in Chile in decades. The bill still must return to the Chamber of Deputies and faces challenges at Chile's Constitutional Tribunal (the rough equivalent of the Supreme Court reviewing constitutionality). The government wants the law final before September to factor it into the annual budget.

The reform also eliminates annual property taxes for homeowners over 65 — a popular measure that could strain municipal budgets. Critics on the left call the corporate cut a giveaway to large companies; backers say it will unlock stalled foreign investment in a country that has seen sluggish growth and the highest unemployment rate in South America (9.4%).

A Total Solar Eclipse Is Crossing the Planet Today — Stream It from Santiago Starting at 10:30am

The year's most dramatic sky event passes over Earth today, August 12: a total solar eclipse whose path of totality runs from northern Russia through Greenland, Iceland, and ends over northern Spain and Portugal. Santiago won't see it directly — but the European Space Agency is streaming full coverage free online starting at 10:30am Santiago time (16:30 CEST). If you have kids, it's a good excuse to pull up the livestream and talk about why the moon fits so precisely over the sun. The next eclipse visible from Chile will be an annular ("ring of fire") eclipse on February 6, 2027.

"Chile's New Political Map Has Four Ecosystems — Not a Left-Right Spectrum"

A BioBioChile column argues that the old left-center-right framework no longer captures Chilean political reality. Instead, the writer maps four distinct "ecosystems," each with its own voter base, internal logic, and leadership networks — more like the fragmented parliamentary systems of Europe than the US two-party binary. The piece identifies Kast's hard right, a center-right modernizer bloc, a fractured center-left, and a far-left ecosystem struggling to find relevance under a right-wing government. The argument is that successful politics in Chile now requires building cross-ecosystem coalitions rather than winning a single ideological lane.

Ex-Ante · Center-right policy analysis

"Ending Property Taxes for Seniors: Who Wins, and Which Municipalities Will Pay?"

Ex-Ante examines the megarreforma's popular but complicated provision eliminating annual property taxes (contribuciones) for homeowners over 65 — a measure that passed the Senate this month. The column praises it as overdue relief for middle-class retirees squeezed between fixed pensions and rising property values, but raises pointed questions about which municipalities (especially lower-income comunas that depend heavily on property-tax revenue) will take the biggest hit and how the promised compensation formula will actually work. Think of it as the Chilean version of debating homestead exemptions for retirees in states like Florida or Texas — popular in the suburbs, complicated at the city-budget level.
