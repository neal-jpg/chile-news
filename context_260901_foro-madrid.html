What Is Foro Madrid?

Foro Madrid is an international network of right-wing and conservative political parties, founded in Madrid in 2020 and organized by Fundación Disenso, a think tank linked to Spain's Vox party — roughly the Spanish equivalent of the MAGA movement, though with older roots in Spanish nationalism. Its founding document, the Carta de Madrid, frames itself as a defense of freedom and democracy against "Bolivarian narco-dictatorships" (socialist and left-leaning governments) in Latin America.

Critics argue the Carta also contains principles incompatible with liberal democracy: it expresses deep skepticism of independent courts, media independence, and social and minority rights. Jurists on the left and center, including former constitutional judges, have flagged it as a document that treats democracy as a tool to be used selectively rather than a universal framework.

Each year Foro Madrid holds a Regional Meeting, gathering leaders and activists from over 25 countries across Europe and the Americas. Chile is hosting the 5th Regional Meeting on September 3–4, 2026 — the first time a sitting Chilean president has opened the event. President José Antonio Kast signed the Carta de Madrid in 2021, when he was still an opposition congressman and presidential candidate.

American analogy: Imagine CPAC (Conservative Political Action Conference) going international and being hosted — with a presidential speech — on the grounds of the White House, with Viktor Orbán (Hungary's illiberal prime minister) and Giorgia Meloni (Italy's post-fascist PM) as co-featured speakers. That's roughly the ideological register of Foro Madrid, with similar controversy.

The event has split Chile's governing coalition. Evópoli and Renovación Nacional — the two center-right parties that joined Kast's coalition (think: establishment Republicans uncomfortable with MAGA) — officially distanced themselves from Foro Madrid, saying it doesn't represent their political vision. Kast's own Partido Republicano embraced it.

The opposition has organized a counter-event called Democracia Siempre (Democracy Always) for the same day, bringing together parties from the Communist Party to Christian Democracy to publicly reject the Foro's framing of democracy. Separate tributes to President Allende — whose socialist government was overthrown in the 1973 coup — are also scheduled for the week of September 11, Chile's coup anniversary.
